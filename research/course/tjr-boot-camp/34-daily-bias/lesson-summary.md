## What TJR teaches

### Why daily bias matters
- The biggest key is NOT executing purely off the execution strategy, but also playing into your daily bias — understanding where the market wants to go for the day `[00:20]`, `[00:25]`.
- If you take a trade opposite the direction you think price will go for the day, odds are it'll be a very short-lived trade or you'll just lose it `[00:34]`, `[00:37]`, `[00:40]`.
- If price goes the opposite direction and you get stopped out, that means your daily bias was wrong `[00:10]` (~10:10), `[00:14]` (~14:12).
- Being proved wrong is acceptable IF you followed your plan/bias — market broke structure / shifted market structure and just wanted to change direction `[00:13]` (~13:00)–`[00:23]` (~13:23).

### The core method: top-down analysis
Start on the highest timeframe and scale down `[00:45]`, `[00:47]`.

1. **Weekly timeframe** — figure out where price wants to go for the WEEK `[00:47]`, `[00:51]`, `[00:53]`.
   - Easiest method: literally just look at recent price action / the line chart — is it going up or down? Up = bullish `[00:55]`–`[01:03]`, `[01:43]`, `[03:50]`.
   - The weekly / line chart only gives WEEKLY bias; TJR does NOT really care about it because he trades day-to-day, not swing. A swing trader SHOULD care `[11:41]`, `[11:46]`.

2. **Daily timeframe** — this is where daily bias is actually determined. Look at current daily MARKET STRUCTURE: are we in an uptrend or a downtrend? `[02:02]`, `[02:04]`, `[02:11]`, `[11:30]`, `[11:34]`, `[11:36]`.
   - A break of structure (BOS) to the downside on the daily → bearish; TJR was bullish before that candle, "not anymore" `[02:19]`, `[02:22]`, `[02:24]`, `[02:26]`.
   - BOS to the upside on the daily → bullish `[08:42]`, `[08:44]`.
   - Rule of thumb: figure out what the daily timeframe is saying (e.g., "on gold bearish") → ideal trades are in that direction `[05:25]`, `[05:31]`, `[05:34]`.

3. **4-hour timeframe** — TJR also looks at the 4H trend `[03:01]`, `[03:03]`.
   - Confluence is strongest when 4H and daily agree (e.g., both bearish) `[03:13]`, `[03:16]`.
   - If daily is bearish but 4H is still bullish, you can still look for bearish bias on the hourly (because daily says down), BUT you must be tentative with take-profits `[05:11]`, `[05:13]`, `[05:19]`, `[05:22]`.
   - You CANNOT target the "absurd long-timeframe take profits" until the 4H is proved to the downside (turns bearish); until 4H turns bearish you can't target those `[04:57]`, `[04:59]`, `[05:04]`, `[05:06]`, `[05:08]`. (Details deferred to the take-profit video `[05:01]`.)
   - When daily is bullish, a 4H break of structure to the DOWNSIDE = a retracement on the daily = "prime time" for a setup (a daily retrace) `[09:01]`, `[09:06]`, `[09:09]`, `[09:11]`, `[09:14]`, `[13:50]`, `[13:53]`.

4. **1-hour timeframe** — where TJR finds daily bias / execution `[02:04]`, `[02:06]`; scale to the hourly to find high-confluence areas and execute `[03:18]`, `[03:20]`. Look for bearish (or bullish) confirmation on the hourly `[04:26]`, `[04:50]`.

5. **Scale further (15m, 5m)** — find execution points / more confirmation: 15-minute breaker structure, 5-minute breaker structure, 5-minute order block, liquidity sweep on the 15m, break of structure on the 5m `[10:53]`, `[14:08]`, `[14:14]`, `[14:16]`, `[14:19]`.

### Session timing
- The MAJORITY of the daily moves on the S&P happen during the New York session; everything else is chop `[05:39]`, `[05:41]`, `[05:46]`, `[05:49]`.
- The big up/down moves are caused by the New York session `[05:53]`, `[05:57]`.
- Because daily candle moves come off these big session moves, your trade will very likely be the majority of that daily candle `[07:57]`, `[08:00]`, `[08:03]`, `[08:06]`.
- The intra-day move for the S&P happens during New York Stock Exchange open `[08:33]`, `[08:36]`, `[08:38]`, `[10:28]`.
- You'd want to trade WITHIN the bias, since NY session drives the move `[05:59]`, `[06:01]`.
- Rhetorical: "Who's trading the S&P during London session?" — a bearish daily setup forming on the hourly during London is NOT traded then; it's a high-timeframe trade that takes a long time `[15:09]`, `[15:12]`, `[15:14]`, `[15:17]`, `[15:20]`, `[15:22]`.

### The mental model
- To find daily bias, imagine you're trying to take a trade on the HIGH timeframe (daily / 4H / 1H) `[13:40]`, `[14:26]`, `[14:29]`, `[17:07]`, `[17:09]`.
- Ask: where would I take the trade? Where are my building blocks confirming the daily bias? Then scale down and find MORE execution points / more reasons `[17:14]`, `[17:16]`, `[17:18]`, `[17:21]`, `[17:23]`.
- Building blocks / execution points used as confirmation: order block, fair value gap (imbalance), equilibrium, liquidity / draw on liquidity, liquidity sweep, break of structure, breaker structure `[02:50]`–`[03:20]`, `[09:19]`–`[10:12]`, `[13:56]`–`[16:37]`.

### Worked flow (bearish, S&P today — Powell/"pal" speaking, TJR did not trade)
Weekly bullish, Daily bearish (breaker/BOS down), 4H bullish (uptrend), 1H "in between" `[03:56]`–`[04:11]`, `[04:13]`. Look for bearish confirmation on the hourly, keep in mind 4H still bullish `[04:24]`–`[05:19]`. Sequence: high gets taken out (liquidity sweep) → break of structure to the downside on the 15m → order block "this is where orders were filled" = elite entry `[06:38]`–`[07:12]`.

### Worked flow (bullish, daily BOS up)
Daily BOS up → 4H look for bullish confirmation; even a 4H BOS down = daily retrace (good) `[08:42]`–`[09:17]`. Price into equilibrium and fair value gap → scale lower → wait for hourly confirmation → hourly BOS up → order block not hit / bearish candle → equilibrium + FVG get hit `[09:19]`–`[10:12]`. Then scale to 15m/5m for building-block execution `[10:50]`–`[11:01]`.

### Full worked setup (bearish, "putting it all together")
S&P: breaker structure down on daily, highs (draw on liquidity) above; go to 4H, sweep those highs; scale to 1H, took out highs; breaker structure on hourly during London (not traded — it's a slow HTF trade); liquidity sweep + break of structure; FVG + order block + equilibrium — maybe enter purely off equilibrium `[14:40]`–`[15:50]`. Targets: 4H retracement / imbalance, 4H equilibrium (from the swing), the leg down that caused the move up `[15:54]`–`[16:24]`. Get precise entry on 5m: wait for a 5-minute break of structure (NOT just a down candle) into the FVG/order block `[16:28]`–`[16:45]`. This becomes a 4H/hourly/daily-confirmed trade taken off a 5-minute execution, allowing daily/hourly/4H take-profits `[16:45]`–`[17:04]`.

### Weekly draws on liquidity (targets)
- Weekly in a "fat ass uptrend" probably needs to draw toward equilibrium; equilibrium marked on the weekly `[18:12]`, `[18:14]`, `[18:17]`, `[18:19]`, `[18:21]`, `[18:23]`.
- If daily is in a downtrend, that means we're retracing on the weekly `[18:25]`, `[18:27]`, `[18:29]`. On the weekly TJR points out FVG, liquidity, and an order block as draws `[18:33]`, `[18:35]`, `[18:41]`, `[18:50]`.

## Codex interpretation (labeled — NOT taught verbatim)

- **codex_interpretation:** TJR's "bias" is direction-of-day derived from HTF market structure (BOS direction) plus a trend eyeball on weekly/daily; this is standard SMC (Smart Money Concepts) top-down analysis. He is not giving a fixed premium/discount % threshold or a numeric equilibrium rule here — "equilibrium" is used qualitatively (50% of a leg is the conventional SMC definition, but he does not state the number in this lesson).
- **codex_interpretation:** "Draw on liquidity" here = prior swing highs/lows (buy-side/sell-side liquidity) that price is likely to reach; TJR uses them both as confirmation of direction and as take-profit targets. He does not enumerate a fixed list of liquidity types (e.g., session highs/lows, PDH/PDL, equal highs/lows) in this lesson.
- **codex_interpretation:** The decision logic can be reduced to: (1) weekly trend (context only, ignored by TJR for day trading); (2) daily market structure = the actual bias (BOS up→bullish, BOS down→bearish); (3) 4H must agree or be in retracement for full-size targets; (4) 1H/15m/5m provide execution confirmation (sweep → BOS → FVG/OB/equilibrium entry); (5) execute during NY session for the S&P.
- **codex_interpretation:** "Powell / pal speaking" — high-impact macro news (FOMC / Fed chair) as a no-trade condition. TJR says he didn't trade because of it but does not state a general news-avoidance rule in this lesson.
- **codex_interpretation:** No explicit numeric thresholds, no stop-loss / RR / lot-size rules, and no exact session clock times (in a stated timezone) are given in this lesson — see verification-questions.md and ambiguities.md.
