This is **Day 1 of a 3-day Order Block (OB) building-block series** [01:38]. TJR

## What TJR teaches (faithful to transcript)

### Definition of an Order Block
- An OB ties directly back to the prior liquidity-sweep lesson. "In order for
  there to be a liquidity sweep… we have to either take out a high or a low…
  by taking out a high there has to be a move up and by taking out a low there
  has to be a move down… prior to the break of structure that ends up shifting
  the market structure" [02:45–03:07].
- **His core definition (repeated several times):** "essentially all an order
  block is is that move up that causes the liquidity sweep" [03:10–03:15].
  Restated at the end: "it is the move prior to the liquidity sweep that causes
  the liquidity sweep" [09:49–09:55].
- Later, asking where the OB is on a bearish (down) example: "**it's the leg
  down prior to the liquidity sweep**" [08:55–09:01]. So bullish OB = the leg /
  move up that sweeps a low and breaks structure up; bearish OB = the leg down
  that sweeps a high and breaks structure down.
- **Why it's called an "order block":** "when liquidity gets swept orders get
  filled… that's why it's called an order block" [03:17–03:20]. The OB is
  "this whole price range… where the orders were filled" [03:24–03:31]. He also
  calls it an **"accumulation area"** — "that's where orders were accumulated…
  I call them like accumulation areas… pretty much the same thing" [11:09–11:20].

### How the OB behaves (the "why it works")
- Market makers filled orders within that price range on the initiating leg
  [04:40–04:48]. **Analogous to a fair value gap (FVG):** "similarly to how
  fair value gaps… they want to push price back within this price range… to
  fill more orders" [04:52–05:07].
- On a retracement back **into** the OB, more resting orders "waiting to be
  filled in the opposite direction" get filled, sending price further in the
  trend direction [05:34–05:45, 05:56–06:09, 10:08–10:19].

### OB as an entry / point of interest (POI)
- Ranking for entries: the **liquidity sweep + break of structure IS the start
  of the move / "the top of the entry"** [06:18–06:26]. FVGs, OBs, and
  equilibrium are used for **retracement entries** [06:28–06:34].
- If you miss the liquidity-sweep + BOS entry, you have **three retracement
  options** [06:45–06:57]. **His #1 choice every time is the order block**
  [06:57–07:01], "because it's still at the top of the move and oftentimes you
  can get an even better entry than off just the liquidity sweep and the break
  of structure" [07:04–07:13]. Priority order stated: **OB first, then fair
  value gap, then equilibrium** [07:44–07:47]. "FVG and equilibrium are pretty
  similar" [07:47–07:50].
- The **low is often the very beginning of the OB**, so a retrace can fill you
  near there [07:13–07:20].

### Key rule — one OB per trend
- "**There's only one order block within a whole trend**" [07:53–08:01], because
  OBs form off a liquidity sweep + orders filled + start of a new trend
  [08:04–08:11]. A prior uptrend gets broken by the sweep/BOS, leaving one OB;
  price may retrace up into it, then the new downtrend begins; **no other OBs
  form within that trend** — only FVGs and equilibrium get filled inside it
  [08:11–08:43].
- A **new OB is only made on the next trend shift** [08:46–08:55].
- **Fractal:** "there can be order blocks on every single time frame" — the
  concept applies on every timeframe (he cites 1-minute) [09:23–09:35, 10:50–10:57].

### Process / news note (tangent)
- At the end he shows a live chart: **PPI and FOMC** were in play, FOMC "in
  about two hours" [11:58–12:04]. On the **15-minute** chart, since the U.S.
  open GBPJPY had moved only **34 pips** — an "extremely volatile pair" — as
  evidence **not to trade on high-impact news days** [12:20–12:48].

  candle before displacement." Standard SMC defines a bullish OB as the last
  down-candle before an up displacement (and vice versa). TJR's "leg down prior
  to the liquidity sweep" language is consistent with that but he never states
  the single-candle rule or whether to use one candle vs the whole leg. → high
  ambiguity, see ambiguities.md.

  (incl. wick), nor high/low measurement. → ambiguities.md, confidence low.
- **Mitigation (NOT defined):** he never uses "mitigated," "valid/invalid," or

  ambiguities.md.
