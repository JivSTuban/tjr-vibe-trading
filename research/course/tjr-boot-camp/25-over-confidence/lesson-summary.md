# Boot Camp Day 25: Over Confidence — Summary

**Primary topic:** psychology (discipline / risk-behavior guardrails)
**Transcript source:** YouTube auto-captions (en-orig ASR), ~18 min. Heavy profanity/rant; a lot of it is a repeated warning against overtrading, but it yields several hard behavioral rules.

## What TJR teaches

**Core thesis:** Trading is played *purely off probabilities* `[03:48]–[04:05]`. The mistake after a winning trade is getting "back on the charts" to search for another trade to turn profits into "something even more" — that greedy/overconfident mindset lowers your probability `[03:31]–[03:47]`.

**Best trade of the day is the FIRST one (price + time theory)** `[04:16]–[04:56]`:
- At/near **market open** is when you are "most probable," because that is when **new money comes into the market** — you usually see a **liquidity sweep**, orders get filled, and price is pushed in its intended direction.
- That's why he says take **"one to two trades a day, one trade is best."** After the first trade, **volume dies out**; you can no longer catch the top/bottom of the move, only retracements and lower-RR trades that take longer to develop.

**Overtrading / revenge-trading trap** `[05:15]–[06:23]`:
- Chasing "that one trade to get you out of the hole" you dug is worse than gambling — "you're literally better off putting it all on black." You force a low-probability trade you haven't thought out, imposing your bias on a market that "isn't primed for it," often with high leverage — and it's typically your 5th–6th trade of the day.

**The confidence band (between fear and overconfidence)** `[07:56]–[08:29]`:
- There is a narrow line: on one side **overconfidence** (over-risk, over-leverage, over-trade); on the other side **fear/under-confidence** (miss your entry, under-leverage, wait too long to pull the trigger). "Be confident enough to take the trade, not overconfident enough to over-risk/over-leverage/over-trade, and not scared enough to miss your entry."

**Kill overconfidence with brutal self-honesty** `[06:37]–[07:14]`, `[18:35]`:
- Repeated mantra: **"You are not profitable. Put it in your head that you are not profitable. You're here to learn the skill."** This mindset "kills that overconfidence immediately" and makes you open to taking losses.

**Win-streak danger** `[09:39]–[10:15]`:
- On a win streak TJR says he "almost encourages a loss" because it proves he's "human again"; wins get in your head fast. **A loss is always coming — even on a streak.** Once you *have* proven profitability, **don't change what got you there** (risk management, no over-leverage, no over-trading) `[10:58]–[11:22]`.

**Demo-account rule for the fearful/unproven** `[11:54]–[12:10]`:
- If you're fearful or losing a lot, you "clearly aren't there yet" — **use a demo account**, master yourself and build confidence on demo, but **don't get too confident on demo and jump to a live account and start over-leveraging** (TJR says this happened to him: profitable on demo → upped leverage on live → got screwed) `[12:29]–[12:56]`.

**News / high-impact events** `[16:13]–[18:34]`:
- "**I don't trade news**." He warns the boot camp not to trade **tomorrow and Thursday** because **Fed Chair Powell is speaking** — likely the most volatile days. Today (no news) everyone won because conditions were high-probability. Beginners who trade the Powell days will win by luck (dangerous — breeds overconfidence) or lose big. Explicit **"don't trade tomorrow and don't trade on Thursday."**

**One-to-two-trades quantification** `[16:56]–[17:11]`: "Your mentors take one to two trades a day — you need to do the same."

He also jabs at ICT "white-labeling" strategies (Silver Bullet is "just a fair value gap") `[14:40]–[15:27]` — the point being that chasing a "new Holy Grail strategy" is itself an overconfidence trap that leads to overtrading.

## Codex interpretation (labeled inference toward machine rules)

- **Max trades per session/day = 1 (soft), 2 (hard cap).** Concrete and repeated ("one trade is best," "one to two trades a day"). This is a clean risk-engine guardrail: after N filled trades in a session, block new entries. Confidence high on the cap; the "1 is best" is a preference.
- **Prefer the market-open / first-setup trade.** Score/weight setups higher near session open (new-money liquidity sweep window); down-weight or skip later low-volume retracement setups. TJR gives the *rationale* but no exact clock time or session name here — treat the open window as the same session/kill-zone defined in other lessons; time value unknown in THIS lesson.
- **News blackout guardrail.** "I don't trade news" + explicit "don't trade [Powell] tomorrow/Thursday." Codify: suppress entries on high-impact event days (FOMC / Fed-chair speeches, and by extension CPI/PPI/NFP). No general list or window is defined here — pin it from a dedicated news lesson. Confidence medium (the *don't-trade-news* stance is clear; the exact event set/window is not).
- **Post-win / win-streak cool-down.** Don't increase risk after wins; don't hunt a second trade to "compound." Enforce fixed risk regardless of recent P/L and enforce the trade cap even (especially) on streaks.
- **No revenge / no size-up after loss.** Never take an unplanned trade to recover a loss; never up leverage. Fixed risk %, no martingale.
- **Demo-gate for unproven strategies/accounts.** Not directly a live-engine rule, but supports a "must prove profitability over a sample before scaling size/going live" gate.
- **NOTE:** No explicit risk-% number is stated in this lesson (he says "risk management," "don't over-risk," references a "5K account" losing "a couple grand" as a *bad* example, but gives no prescribed per-trade risk %). Do not invent one.
