# Lesson 04 — Trends

## What TJR teaches

Day 4, framed as "another trading day" after candlesticks (Day 1) and two discipline talks [00:00]. Topic: **Trends**. TJR calls it a "super basic simple" recap-level subject but insists these fundamentals are "the massive umbrella" over strategy and execution — you cannot master execution if you don't know where the trend is going [00:33-01:04].

**Why trend matters.** "Why do we even care about the trend — because it dictates where the market is going." Markets move off momentum: once a market starts trending, "odds are it's going to continue in that direction" [01:09-01:22]. Analogy: a basketball pushed on a flat surface keeps rolling because of momentum; the market gets a "little push or a market structure shift" and starts trending [01:29-01:53].

**Definition of an uptrend.** When market is trending higher "it moves in higher highs and higher lows" [02:20-02:35]. Mechanically: when price pushes past a prior high it "makes a new high that's higher than the previous one, and it makes a new low that's higher than the previous one" — higher highs and higher lows — and this "continues all the way until it breaks its trend" [02:51-03:12].

**Definition of a downtrend.** "The exact opposite" — "lower highs and lower lows." Each low is lower than the previous, each high is lower than the previous, "and it continues" [03:14-03:49]. Restated later: in a downtrend the highs are not going higher; in an uptrend the lows are not going lower [05:21:00-05:28]. He also observes that in a trend the impulse legs (with-trend) are larger than the retracement legs: "the moves higher are always smaller than the moves lower" in a downtrend, "if you measure it in every single trend" [05:20:00-05:21:00].

**Break of structure preview.** On a daily chart he walks live price: high → low → higher high → higher low (repeated), then "higher high, now lower low because we just surpassed this" → "it broke structure … it's probably going to flip its direction" [04:41-05:04]. Once structure broke and direction changed, price "continued that for a while," and after the next break "odds are we're going to start trending in the opposite direction" [05:12-05:25]. He explicitly defers the mechanics: "the next trading video will jump into break of structure … I might even do two trading videos on that" [06:59-07:15]. **This lesson is the swing-point foundation that Lesson 06 (Break of Structure) depends on.**

**Trade WITH the trend.** "Going against the trend is never smart" — he names it his own biggest struggle, trading against trend/bias [05:07:00-05:09:00]. "The trend is your friend." Why fight momentum: if you want to reach the end of a road, take the car going downhill (momentum carries you) not uphill [06:13-06:41]. Don't try to catch retracements — you don't know how deep a retracement goes, but the extension "always goes past the high," high after high gets pushed through [06:52-07:18]. So follow the wave/momentum because "that's where price is going to go either way" [06:46-06:52].

**Multi-timeframe rule (HTF authority).** People "get so caught up in all these different time frames," seeing "a hundred different trend switches" — "probably a couple thousand" — on the 15-minute inside one weekly uptrend, and that's where they go wrong [07:41-08:15]. **"Higher time frames hold higher power."** If the 4H says bearish, "why do we care what the 15-minute is saying?" Use the 15-minute **in confluence** with the higher trend [08:19-08:40]. Workflow: let the 4H / daily / weekly define direction, then **scale down to lower time frames for an optimized entry** to catch the large HTF move [08:40-09:21]. Concrete example of the stacked read: liquidity sweep on the 30-minute → break of structure to the downside on the 5-minute, while 4H and daily are "bear bear bear bear" → "we just caught the top of the next leg down" [10:28-10:59]. He clarifies he DOES trade off liquidity sweeps / trend-changes, but on **lower** time frames, used in confluence with HTF bias [10:24-10:59].

**Third market state: consolidation.** "There's three different ways that market moves … either through trends or just consolidation" [12:20-12:39] — enumerated as trends (up/down) plus consolidation, i.e. three states (uptrend, downtrend, consolidation/ranging). Consolidation = a higher high and higher low that "is not a trend"; price "stayed static, stagnant, didn't make a move" until it broke the floor/low and changed market structure into a downtrend [12:39-13:37]. After the break: high → low → lower high → lower low → lower high, "probably a lower low targeting this area of liquidity" [13:29-13:52].

**Line-chart tip.** To simplify trend reading, strip candlestick color and use a **line chart** — "simplify the f*** out of it," just read high-low-high-low / higher-high-higher-low [18:00-18:29, 19:15-19:39]. Some beginners "could be trading like this and be profitable" with just basic trend structure; TJR trades more precisely (optimized entries → tighter stops → higher risk-reward) but the basics alone can work [14:09-14:33].

**Session context (color).** He notes it's Sunday, market is consolidating post-Friday; expects clarity once Asian → London → New York sessions play out [20:07-20:23]. Session times are not defined here.

**Homework.** On your one or two pairs (examples: S&P 500, gold, GJ = GBPJPY, GU = GBPUSD [24:57-25:01]), for the upcoming week: (1) predict where price wants to go for the **week**, journal/write it down; (2) scale to the **daily** and predict the next couple of days; (3) predict **today**. Use the TradingView **forecast tool** to drag from current price to your target — no trade, no stop loss, no position, just a directional projection [23:59-27:07]. Focus on ONE or TWO pairs only, not 10-20 [27:11-27:38]. Recommends experimenting with the line chart [27:47-28:00].

## Codex interpretation

- **Uptrend** = sequence of higher highs (HH) AND higher lows (HL). **Downtrend** = lower highs (LH) AND lower lows (LL). Both defined purely by swing-point structure. Trend persists "until it breaks its trend" (i.e. until a swing point fails to extend / a counter-swing is broken → Break of Structure, Lesson 06).
- **Consolidation/ranging** is the third state: swing points that do NOT progressively extend (static/stagnant), bounded by a floor and ceiling, until one boundary breaks and a trend (re)starts. This is effectively the negation of the trend condition.
- **HTF dominance** is a hard directional filter: the higher timeframe (4H/daily/weekly) sets the permitted trade direction; lower timeframes (30m/15m/5m) are only for entry timing "in confluence," never for direction against HTF. The stacked chain implied for entries — HTF bias (down) + LTF liquidity sweep (30m) + LTF BOS (5m) → enter with HTF — is a confirmation-gated reversal-at-extreme, matching Lesson 08's liquidity model. All stages load-bearing.
- **Trade-with-trend** is a directional gate, not an entry rule: never counter-trend, never fade a retracement mid-trend; bias toward catching the next impulse ("leg") in the HTF direction.
- The swing-point vocabulary here (HH/HL/LH/LL, "break of structure," "market structure shift," "surpassed this high/low") is the definitional substrate for Lesson 06 (BOS). BOS = the moment a trend's defining swing sequence is violated (a HL broken in an uptrend, or an LH broken in a downtrend), flipping direction. TJR previews but does not formalize the break threshold here.
- Unspecified / not code-able from this lesson: which swing points qualify (no fractal/lookback definition), how far price must exceed a high/low to "push through" or "break structure" (ticks/pips/%), exact timeframe pairings, and session clock times. All deferred.
