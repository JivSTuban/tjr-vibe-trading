# Lesson 03 — Discipline

## What TJR teaches

Day 3 is the first pure discipline/psychology lesson. TJR states the whole course will **alternate between trading and discipline day-by-day** — "first day discipline, second day trading, today discipline, tomorrow trading" — because his biggest issue with other courses is they under-prioritize psychology, giving it "just one little baby section" [01:23-01:47]. There is intentionally almost no trading content today [03:16-03:20].

**The core thesis.** He claims the ONLY difference between him (profitable) and the student (not yet profitable) is **mindset** — "yes I do have more years in the market but technically we are trading the same strategy... sometimes you guys are bailing out of a trade... sometimes you aren't confident enough to take those trades" [03:34-03:57]. Those confidence/bailing holes are what discipline plugs. He asserts the trading part is "relatively easy" [04:03].

**Homework framing.** Most of the video describes required daily homework [03:16-03:23]. The assignment: **pick ONE single habit** — something you've always wished you'd do but never had the discipline to — and complete it **every single day**, starting the moment the video ends [04:57-05:12, 08:37-09:00]. It must be OUTSIDE your comfort zone: if you already go to the gym daily you are NOT allowed to pick the gym, because "this has to be something that you are uncomfortable doing" [07:57-08:08]. Start small — "walk down into the kiddie pool," not the deep end; he explicitly says do the OPPOSITE of David Goggins' 100-mile run — do a **one-mile walk** or one uncomfortable-but-beneficial thing [07:39-07:47, 21:14-21:31]. TJR models it himself: he committed to **reading 15-20 pages a day**, starting Day 1 of the course, and has hit it every day [08:48-09:36].

**The habit-formation numbers (verbatim).** He cites a statistic: a **habit forms over ~21 days**, but a **routine/ritual takes ~60 days** to become natural/comfortable [09:40-10:15]. Go to the gym 21 days straight and you'll likely go on day 22, but it isn't truly habitual/routine until ~day 60 [09:56-10:15].

**The motivation-vs-discipline curve.** Day 1 you feel super motivated; day 2 you already question it; by the end of **week 1 (day 7-8)** motivation is gone [10:52-11:30, 27:07-27:18]. "Motivation starts to dwindle... once you get to that weak point the motivation is gone and the discipline is all that you have left" [27:07-27:18]. The only thing that stops progress is **giving up** — quitting after 7-8 days makes you "a loser" [20:16-21:05]. He frames the closing rule bluntly: "the only way you can get there is not through motivation but discipline" [30:23-30:35].

**Accountability mechanic.** TJR says he will **check in every other discipline video** (not daily) on whether you kept the habit, because it's easy to stay motivated for two days [26:53-27:06, 27:19-27:31]. Guilt lever: skip a day and "somebody else was working that day... somebody else was working harder than you" [31:31-31:57].

**"1% better every day."** The stated goal for the whole course/summer is to get **one percent better every single day** — many little compounding habits, not one switch you flip once [26:14-26:24].

**Dream-self modeling.** A recurring tactic: imagine the exact person you want to become (body, face, habits, daily activities), figure out **what they do daily to maintain and keep improving** (not just how they got there), and copy that [24:14-25:32]. Trading parallel he draws: professional traders trade with **risk management, no emotion, discipline** — and you "can't treat this like a casino, you can't be over-trading, over-leveraging, because you'll never be profitable if you do that" [24:56-25:10].

**Extra optional homework.** Take **front + back shirtless mirror photos** now to track improvement (he took weekly pics as a "fat ass" and was "shredded with abs" by 3 months) [22:39-23:11]. And optionally **document the journey daily on TikTok** ("day one of doing TJR's boot camp...") as a public-accountability + subconscious-programming device [31:44-33:33]. Both are labeled optional ("you don't have to") [28:26-28:29].

Closing homework restated: **choose one beneficial habit outside your comfort zone, keep it all summer / for life, then turn the video off and go do it** [29:43-31:36].

## Codex interpretation

- This lesson is psychology but yields concrete behavioral guardrails. The load-bearing numbers to encode as parameters: **21-day habit threshold**, **~60-day routine/ritual threshold**, **day 7-8 motivation cliff**, **1%/day improvement target**, **1 habit minimum**, **every-other-lesson check-in cadence**, **1-mile walk floor / weekly progress photos**.
- Direct trading guardrails stated in passing (encode as proposed rules): **no over-trading, no over-leveraging, trade with fixed risk management, remove emotion, execute the plan with discipline** [24:56-25:10]. These are asserted here but the concrete numbers (max trades, max leverage/risk %) are NOT given in this lesson — defer to the risk-management/loss lessons.
- The "only difference is mindset / bailing early / not confident enough to take trades" claim [03:34-03:57] maps to two machine-relevant behaviors: (a) do not exit a valid trade early out of fear, (b) do not skip a valid signal out of low confidence. Both need an objective "valid setup" definition from the strategy lessons before they're testable — flagged in ambiguities.
- Everything about self-image, comfort zone, "boy into a man," girls/confidence is motivational context, not a rule.
- The 21/60-day and "habit forms in 21 days" figures are pop-psychology heuristics TJR half-hedges ("I believe it's 21 days"); treat as his stated targets, not validated facts. Confidence medium.
