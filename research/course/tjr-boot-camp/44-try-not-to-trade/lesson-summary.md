# Lesson 44 — Try Not To Trade

## What TJR teaches

Core thesis: **know when NOT to trade a pair.** The lesson is a set of filters that
disqualify a trade, plus the psychology behind trading less.

**1. Setup must align with daily bias** [02:17–03:20]. Weekly analysis and pair selection are "interlocked" with daily bias — if you have a firm daily bias you can choose a trade in line with it, and that in turn makes it a good trade. The *first step is making sure the setup is in line with your daily bias.* Example of a GOOD trade: S&P bullish daily → 4H breaks structure up → takes out an hourly building block → 5m break of structure confirms → that's a trade he'd take. BAD trade: daily downtrend but 4H up, 1H up, not near any building block, no LTF confirmation, "everyone thing just saying bullish" on an overall bearish-bias pair — low probability, avoid [03:31–04:13].

**2. Don't trade directionless / choppy price** [04:56–08:28]. "When there's no sense of direction in the market" — a "whole bunch of nothing" — is a sign in itself to not trade. Chop that keeps sweeping liquidity and faking breaks of structure ("psych, liquidity sweep, falls … no break of structure, break of structure the upside, psych, sweep you again") is "not fun to read" and "you're not going to be able to take a trade off of that." **The highest-probability markets are trending markets** — a clean higher-high/higher-low uptrend is "very easy price action to read"; chop is not [06:28–07:16].

**3. Don't switch a decision on a lower-TF move** [09:57–13:09]. "Higher time frames hold higher power." A 5m break of structure could just be a 15m retrace; a 15m break of structure could be an hourly retrace. Decide where price wants to go on the day / 4H / 1H, then execute on lower timeframes *to fulfill that*, as long as it's within the daily bias. If none of those checkpoints are hit → **don't trade this pair today, or don't trade today** [13:01–13:09].

**4. Trade fewer days on purpose** [09:48–11:31]. "Stop trying to force a trade on a pair when you can cycle over to another one, and if you're only trading one pair, don't trade that day." **"The best traders take the most days off in the market."** He doesn't trade news to *increase probability*. Rationale: "the more days you are in this market the higher probability it is that you will lose, statistically speaking" — the market is built to beat you, so don't add low-probability trades to an already low-probability market. Cites "Paladin" taking ~8 trades in a month. Preference: a few super-solid high-probability in-line trades over 20 mediocre against-bias trades chasing a dopamine rush [11:13–11:41]. Prescription: **go into the market not wanting to trade** and make the market give you no other option but to take the trade because you know it will make money [11:41–12:07].

**5. Review your losses on the chart** [24:41–26:00]. Trading has "game film" built in — the chart history "tells you exactly why you lost" and where price actually wanted to go. If you know market structure, go back after a loss and identify the trade you *should* have taken. "That's the backtesting you need to do — live demo trading, then journaling / trade reviews … writing it down puts it deep into your head."

The back half [~13:58 onward] is a motivation tangent (work harder than everyone, encourage failure, "it's not *I want*, it's *I am*," hold yourself accountable) — process guardrails rather than mechanical rules.

## Codex interpretation

- This lesson is a **trade-suppression filter stack**. Machine encoding: a candidate setup must pass ALL of — (a) direction aligns with daily bias; (b) market is trending, not chop; (c) confirmation exists on the intended execution TF; (d) no HTF checkpoint is contradicted. Fail any → suppress.
- **Anti-overtrading guardrails** convert cleanly to behavioral limits: prefer no-trade over forced trade; cycle instruments before forcing; if only one instrument and it fails filters, take the day off; treat "days in market" as a cost. A per-day / per-session max-trades cap and a "one qualifying setup per session" default are consistent with the teaching (exact numbers NOT given here — Paladin's ~8/month is anecdotal, not a rule).
- **Chop detector** needed: quantify "directionless" (e.g., repeated failed BOS / liquidity sweeps within a range, low net displacement). TJR describes it qualitatively; thresholds must be defined and confirmed.
- **HTF-dominance rule** (recurs in 43, 47, 49): never let a lower-TF structure event override a higher-TF-derived decision; treat it as a retrace of the higher TF.
