## What TJR teaches

### The top-down daily bias workflow (core of the lesson)

**Step 1 — Weekly time frame: find overall direction.** Start on the weekly to figure out overall direction [01:04][01:07]. He says you can even use a line chart [01:13]. Example: the S&P is moving up, breaking structure to the upside on the weekly, about to break structure on the monthly to the upside [01:16][01:23]. His "next draw on liquidity is up here" with another draw noted [01:47][01:51]. He notes the weekly just gives you an overview/overall direction before scaling down [05:41][05:43].

**Step 2 — Daily time frame: find daily bias / daily trend.** "We are trying to base our trades based off of daily candles" [02:57]. Understand daily market structure / daily trend → then you know your daily bias and where you'd ideally want to take a trade [03:03][03:06][03:09]. In an uptrend you want to catch the extension (higher highs / higher lows), not the retracement, because "the extension is always way larger and way bigger than the retracement" [03:19][03:27][03:29]. Ideally catch Longs in an uptrend [03:36].

**Draw on liquidity as bias input.** He took shorts yesterday because "we swept daily liquidity up here" [03:48][03:50]. He asks where the market is likely to draw toward — e.g., toward highs where liquidity rests, and to fill an imbalance [09:24][09:26][09:28][09:31].

**Step 3 — Retracement tools when daily is retracing.** On Gold: weekly bearish, daily bearish, currently retracing on the daily = "even better" [08:53][08:56][08:59]. Retracement tools = fair value gap + equilibrium [09:01]. Find equilibrium; "we're not yet in a discount" [09:03][09:07]. A fair value gap sitting within a discount is a confluence [09:10][09:12]. Everything above the 50% mark he calls a premium ("everything above this 50 mark") [14:37][14:40].

**Step 4 — Scale down through building blocks in line with bias.** After daily direction, find a "building block area of high confluence within our bias" — order block, fair value gap, imbalance [06:59][07:31][09:49]. Then scale down and look for the SAME building block entries on lower time frames [07:40][07:42]. The whole higher-timeframe zone is a "high confidence price zone" to trade in your bias direction [07:50][07:52].

**Step 5 — Lower-timeframe execution sequence (the entry model).**
- Look for liquidity sweeps on the 15-minute → break of structure on the 15-minute → order block entry on the 15-minute [07:09][07:11][07:14][07:16].
- Scale down to the 5-minute → break of structure → "sniper entry" [07:16][07:17][07:19][07:20].
- On the S&P specifically: "come into this imbalance breaking structure on the 5-minute, order block entry with a break of structure on the 1-minute — boom, long" [12:31][12:33][12:35][12:38].
- Preferred execution frames: Forex on the 15-minute and 5-minute; S&P on the 5-minute and 1-minute [11:45][11:47][11:50][11:55].

**Confluence stack when everything aligns.** S&P looks great for longs because hourly, 4-hour, daily, weekly, and 15-minute are ALL in an uptrend [13:10][13:13][13:16][13:18]. When aligned, wait for price points to get filled and a break of structure to the upside on a lower timeframe for confirmation, then enter [13:19][13:22][13:24][13:28].

## Codex interpretation (labeled, NOT taught verbatim)

- **codex_interpretation:** TJR's "draw on liquidity" = the market's likely target — a pool of resting liquidity (prior highs/lows) or an unfilled imbalance/FVG that price is drawn to fill. Bias points toward the nearest untapped draw. He does not give a numeric rule for choosing between multiple draws in this lesson.
- **codex_interpretation:** "Equilibrium" = the 50% level of a leg; above it = premium (sell zone in a downtrend), below it = discount (buy zone in an uptrend). This is standard ICT-style premium/discount. TJR only explicitly says "everything above this 50 mark" [14:40] and "not yet in a discount" [09:07].
- **codex_interpretation:** "Breaker structure" / "break of structure" in the ASR appears to mean a break of structure (BOS) in the entry direction, used as lower-timeframe confirmation after price taps a building block. He uses it interchangeably for confirmation on 15m/5m/1m.
- **codex_interpretation:** The multi-timeframe alignment check (15m→1h→4h→D→W all same direction) functions as a trend-confluence filter; the more frames agree, the higher confidence. TJR states the frames but gives no scoring threshold.
