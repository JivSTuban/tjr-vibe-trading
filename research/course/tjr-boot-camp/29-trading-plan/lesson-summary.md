# Boot Camp Day 29: Trading Plan — Lesson Summary

The lesson opens with an extended comedic skit / intro [00:11]–[02:43]. Actual instruction begins at [02:43].

## What TJR teaches

TJR frames the trading plan as "the first step at turning into a profitable Trader" [02:43] and calls it a "foolproof idiot-proof plan" you build with just a pencil and paper [05:08]–[05:19]. He walks through a **mock-up trading plan** as a checklist of questions you answer and write down as he asks them [05:22]–[05:33]. The stated purpose of the plan: make trading "robotic day in day out… this is how we become emotionless" [06:29], and to "limit extra trades, limit extra risk, limit low probability" [10:52].

### The checklist (item by item)

**1. Risk management — pre-designate your risk** [06:08]–[06:16]
Called "one of the most important things." Pre-designate risk so "we know exactly how much we're going to lose so when we lose it we're not crying" [06:16]–[06:27].

- **1a. Lot-size method** [06:36]–[08:05]: Decide between a **set/predetermined lot size** (what TJR uses — he cycles between three: **high risk, regular risk, low risk** [07:16]–[07:20]) vs a **calculated lot size** (use a lot-size calculator: input account balance, risk %, stop-loss in pips → it outputs a lot size you enter into MetaTrader [07:31]–[07:48]). TJR says beginners "should use a calculated lot size" and "shouldn't use a predetermined lot size because you won't know what you're risking" [07:57]–[08:05].
- **1b. Monetary risk per day** [08:06]: Know the monetary value you're willing to risk each day. TJR's example: **"I am risking $100 a day"** [09:32]–[09:48].
- **1c. Trades per day** [08:11]: **TJR recommends one trade a day** [08:13]. He himself does one, sometimes two, but when he takes two he **de-risks both so combined risk equals one trade's risk** [08:18]–[08:46]. Beginners: "just stick to one for now" [09:41]. Target **1–2 high-probability quality setups**, not 5 [09:04]–[09:17].

**2. When are you trading? — designate your session/time** [10:00]–[11:18]
"Treat this like a job… designate your time" [11:05]. TJR trades the **New York Stock Exchange open (AM session)** and takes a trade **within the first hour and a half**; if he can't find a trade he doesn't take one — but he must be in a trade by then "or else I'm done" [11:15]–[11:30]. He lists session options: Asian (calls it a "terrible session to trade"), London open, London close into NY open, NY AM, NY PM [10:34]–[10:59]. Choose based on your time zone, sleep schedule, and work schedule [11:01]–[11:07].

**3. News rule** [11:30]–[13:35]
Decide whether you trade **on/around news** or avoid it. This excludes high-impact news — "not CPI, not NFP, not FOMC, not PPI" — which he says all traders should avoid; he means regular red/orange-folder news where you read the bias [11:37]–[11:53]. Beginners avoiding news altogether will get "an insane win rate" [12:02]–[12:12]. He gives a self-set example threshold: if news causes a **~5-point spike within a 5-minute candle**, decide in advance whether you trade that day or opt out [12:48]–[13:17] (explicitly labels the number as arbitrary/"me saying this randomly, you determine this yourself" [13:00]). If news breaks the market, "I'm done for the day" [12:45].

**4. What are you trading? — the instrument** [13:35]–[16:31]
Options he lists: S&P 500, NASDAQ, US30, a forex pair, commodities, crypto [13:37]–[13:48]. **Most beginners should trade only ONE pair** [13:50]. Master one, turn profitable on it, then add another [15:35]–[16:31]. He criticizes trading 10 pairs when you can't master one [14:03]–[14:26]. His own instruments: S&P, gold, GBP/JPY (GJ), GBP/USD (GU) — each traded for years [15:39]–[15:52]. He only added instruments after becoming profitable with just GBP/JPY [16:12]–[16:26]. Action: write your pair down, then delete your whole watchlist of extra pairs [15:04]–[15:22].

**5. How do you enter? — confluences / building blocks** [16:39]–[18:22]
"What are our confluences, all the building blocks?" [16:45]. He says this is hard to finalize now and requires **backtesting** — test a confluence combo (e.g., **liquidity sweep → break of structure**) for ~two weeks, record win rate, record emotions, and **journal** [17:09]–[17:33]. Entry options: enter on just liquidity sweep + BOS, or wait for extra confirmation via a **Fair Value Gap or Order Block entry** for better risk-reward [17:34]–[17:58]. His recommendation for you: be risk-averse and only take a trade **aligned with your daily bias**, with a liquidity sweep and break of structure all aligned with the daily bias and the overall market trend, because that's where the market wants to go = high probability [18:00]–[18:22]. Full entry mechanics deferred to the next lesson ("putting it all together") [16:55]–[17:04], [21:07].

### The decision rule (the "leave" filter) [18:22]–[20:36]
TJR's summary of executing the plan: check the setup against the plan; **if it doesn't follow the plan, leave** [19:45]. Explicit "leave / don't take a trade" conditions:
- Setup doesn't follow your trading plan [19:26]–[19:48].
- You already took your one trade for the day [19:48]–[19:52].
- You already maxed out your pre-designated risk for the day [19:52]–[19:57].
- It's a **US bank holiday** and you can't trade your pair (e.g., S&P 500) [19:57]–[20:05].
- You don't get your confluences (liquidity sweep + break of structure, etc.) [20:16]–[20:22].
- Not during your session (NY open, first 1.5 hrs) [18:41]–[18:45].
- Not your instrument [18:46]–[18:50].
- You haven't figured out your pre-designated risk yet — don't trade until you do [18:50]–[19:00].

"If yes → press the damn button. That's how you take a trade" [19:16]–[19:24]. Follow every step "to the point where you are a robot"; don't enter off FOMO [19:26]–[19:41].

**Homework:** screenshot the mock-up and write your answers next to each question [20:52]–[21:04]; writing up your trading plan is the day's homework [21:03]–[21:07]. Next lesson: "putting it all together" — confluences, building blocks, entries [21:07], [22:20].

## Codex interpretation (labeled — not TJR's exact words)

- The lesson is a **process/checklist framework**, not a mechanical strategy spec. The five checklist items map cleanly to configurable parameters of an automated system: (1) risk model, (2) trading window, (3) news filter, (4) instrument universe, (5) entry confluence stack. The "leave filter" is effectively a set of **hard pre-trade gates** — an AND-chain that must all pass before any order.
- **Codex SMC context** (generic, not stated verbatim here): "liquidity sweep" = price runs a pool of stops/liquidity (BSL/SSL) then reverses; "break of structure" (BOS) = price breaks a prior swing confirming trend continuation; "Fair Value Gap" (FVG) / "Order Block" (OB) = imbalance / last opposing candle used as a refined entry zone; "daily bias" = higher-timeframe directional expectation. TJR's recommended entry = daily-bias-aligned (BSL/SSL sweep → BOS → FVG/OB entry) — a standard trend-continuation ICT/SMC pattern.
- Numbers here are mostly **illustrative and self-set** ($100/day, 5-point/5-min news threshold, ~2-week backtest window). Only "one trade/day" and "first 1.5 hrs of NY open" are given as firm personal rules; treat the rest as user-configurable defaults requiring confirmation.
- The concrete confluence rule ("liquidity sweep + BOS + FVG/OB, aligned with daily bias") is stated but its precise machine definition (timeframes, sweep tolerance, BOS confirmation) is **deferred to the next lesson** and must not be inferred here.
