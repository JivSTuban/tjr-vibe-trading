# Boot Camp Day 27: Fear — Summary

**Primary topic:** psychology (fear/doubt — the opposite pole to Day 25 Over Confidence). Yields the most concrete **risk numbers and account-progression rules** of the psychology set.

## What TJR teaches

**Framing** `[00:56]`, `[19:19]`: Fear and doubt when placing valid trades is the flip side of overconfidence (Day 25). "Psychology is 90% of trading." No strategy can fix it — "it's you versus you," and everyone's psychology is different.

**Step 1 — you shouldn't fear/doubt on DEMO** `[01:56]`–`[06:10]`: If you're new and unprofitable you should *encourage losses* (to learn — see "how to take a loss" video). On demo there is nothing to lose but ego, so there's no rational reason for fear. If you're scared to click on demo, understand it's fake money; lose → learn for free.

**Step 2 — the demo→live confidence gate (CONCRETE)** `[06:57]`–`[07:15]`, `[03:36]`: For TJR personally, the bar to go live is **at least 3 months of profitability, showing 10%+ gain, taking 1 trade a day, risking 1% a day.** Restated `[04:57]`: "nothing is different [on live] besides you risking 1% of your account."

**Step 3 — the "$100 / $1-a-day" ramp** `[10:57]`–`[12:00]`: His biggest advice — **start a $100 live account and risk 1% (~$1 per trade).** Losing $1/day you won't care about → this **trains the emotion** (not excited on wins, not sad on losses) while getting you used to trading REAL money. "You win $2, who cares; you lose $1, who cares."

**Step 4 — real-money odds & money you can afford to lose** `[07:45]`–`[10:02]`: When you jump from demo to a real account, TJR estimates **~90% odds you blow it** the first time because "that money gets to your head" → overleverage/overtrade → old bad habits. **Never put money in a live account you can't afford to lose.** (He calls out people willing to risk 50% of a $2K net worth — "are we being for real?") If you blow it: go back to demo, re-prove profitability, understand *why* you use the strategy and risk management, then return to live.

**Step 5 — funded vs personal capital route** `[12:43]`–`[13:55]`: After 3 months of live profitability, either:
- **Over $10,000** capital you can afford to lose → trade your own live account.
- **Under $10,000** (true for "every single one of you") → **take a funded (prop-firm) challenge.** The **same rules pass a funded challenge: win ~10% in a month.** Pass it, get 1–2 payouts, put the payouts into a live account. That gives ~**4 proof sources / confidence boosters**: demo, demo-again (after blowing the first live), the small live account, and the funded account → "damn near a profitable trader" (~**9 months** of proven profitability).

**Step 6 — trading should be BORING & robotic** `[18:12]`–`[20:06]`: With a proven step-by-step strategy, you have no reason to doubt entries. "Trading should be boring… another uneventful day where I use risk management, take **one trade a day, risk 1%**, stick to my trading plan." Treat it like a **job / step-by-step execution plan**. The problem traders have is coming in every day with a **new strategy and new excitement** → losing → then fearing entries. "Be robotic with this."

**Roadmap** `[15:02]`–`[15:23]`, `[05:02]`: A **trading-plan / how-to-develop-a-trading-plan psychology video** comes by end of week, right before the **"putting everything together" strategy series**. The step-by-step strat is what *gives* you the confidence to enter (because you've proven it's probable/profitable).

- **Account-progression gate (behavioral, not order-flow):** demo → $100 live @1% → (blow/retry loop) → 3-month/10% proof → funded challenge (10%/month) → payouts → live. A safety layer, not a trade trigger; useful as an *account-state machine* around the engine.

- **"Boring/robotic" = the whole thesis for automation:** fixed risk %, fixed max trades, fixed plan, no discretionary "new strategy" chasing. Directly supports a deterministic rule engine.
