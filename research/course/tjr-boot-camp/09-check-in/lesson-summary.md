# Lesson 09 — Check In

## What TJR teaches

A pure psychology/community check-in day — no charts, no trading content [00:11-00:33]. Key points:

- **This is a check-in, not a discipline lecture.** TJR is scouring the comment section to improve the boot camp; asks students to leave questions and requests [00:23-00:35].
- **Cadence & duration.** Week 1 done, entering week 2 — "this is when it's going to get hard, this is when that motivation is going to die down" [00:43-00:46]. He plans to run check-ins "once every other week" / "around two weeks" apart [01:46-01:49, 07:11-07:16], and the whole boot camp is planned to run "probably two months straight of just uploads" [01:56-01:59].
- **Realistic success framing.** With ~20,000 views per boot camp video (his proxy for participants), he expects only "a hundred or maybe even 200 people" to come out profitable [08:05-08:32]. Even after the two months you "definitely won't be profitable" — it's "the time that you put in AFTER this... what you do with this knowledge" that turns you profitable [04:35-04:49].
- **Take it slow.** He deliberately splits liquidity into multiple parts because it's the most valuable/essential concept; analogy to studying for a finals test — don't cram, one concept at a time [02:53-03:15].
- **Rest / anti-burnout.** No homework today. Take a day off, step away from the charts [00:33, 05:52-06:05]. "Give yourself at least one day out of the week to just take a break / mental detox" [06:30-06:37]. Caveat: he does NOT endorse people who party on weekends after doing nothing all week — rest is earned by productive work [06:41-06:57].
- Housekeeping: face cam bought but incompatible with MacBook; a PC arrives ~June 14th [05:01-05:15].

## Codex interpretation

- Yields a behavioral guardrail: **schedule a mandatory rest day (≥1/week)** to prevent burnout and over-analysis ("psych yourself out"). Over-analysis and burnout are named failure modes [06:17-06:22].
- Framing rule: **evaluate profitability on a multi-month/post-course horizon, not per-session.** This aligns with lesson 11/13's "think in yearly %" thread.
- The ~100–200/20,000 success figure is TJR's expectation/motivation, NOT a modelable base rate — do not encode.
