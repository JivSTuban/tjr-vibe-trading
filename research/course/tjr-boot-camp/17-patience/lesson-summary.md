# Lesson 17 — Patience (Boot Camp)

Primary topic: **psychology / trading discipline**. This is a mindset lesson, not a technical setup lesson. No live chart walk-through. TJR's core thesis: impatience is *the* reason retail traders rush in, force trades, and enter without confirmation — and the fix is to make patience structural (rules) rather than something you rely on willpower for.

## What TJR teaches

**Why impatience happens — the dopamine mechanism.** Trading is a "huge dopamine boost" the moment you hit buy or sell [01:39]. He cites a gambling study: the dopamine hit comes at the moment of *placing* the bet, and that hit is bigger than the one from actually winning — so win or lose, pressing the button is the reward the impatient trader is chasing [01:59–02:18]. The danger: you end up thinking only about money you could make, never about probability, percentages, or the money you could lose [01:55–02:39]. Fix that by consciously thinking about the money you could lose [02:39].

**Fix #1 — a step-by-step trading plan removes the patience problem.** "Number one is literally just having a trading plan set up... a step-by-step list of when you should be able to execute" [03:23]. His example sequence: first a **liquidity sweep**, then a **break of structure**, then wait until the **fair value gap gets entered/filled**, then a **reaction**, then enter [03:36–03:49]. "If none of those things apply you don't trade" [03:55]. The plan makes patience automatic — "make it so it's easy to be patient and give yourself rules so that patience doesn't really even have to be anything you think about" [03:59].

**Fix #2 — be emotionless; sitting out is valid.** "Part of the patience game is you are willing to sit out a day" [04:44]. He recommends coming into the market thinking "I do not want to trade today" [05:01]. The market should *give you* a reason to enter — "I shouldn't be making up a reason for me to trade" [05:22]. Wait for the market to come to you; don't force your bias onto it [05:28, 08:01].

**Fix #3 — even a valid setup can be a no-trade.** Sometimes all the checklist items get hit and you still shouldn't trade — e.g. **if there's news, you shouldn't even trade** [06:15–06:22]. "Be open to not trading... in fact, encourage it" [06:22].

**Wait for A+ / high-probability only.** Most days he can find a trade, but students should wait for a "good, high quality, high potential, high probability setup" [03:00–03:08], not enter off one confluence or "oh it looks good" [03:14–03:18]. Want the market to *prove itself* — "this setup is so good there's no reason for you not to take it" [08:36]. Reject the ambivalent setup ("this happened but this didn't, it's against the daily bias, off a retracement — you should not be taking that") [08:43–08:53].

**Micro-patience on entry.** Even after execution points hit, waiting an extra one or two candlesticks won't hurt; wait for price to develop, confirm volume/volatility, then enter [08:18–08:37].

**Frequency & timeframe of mastery.** "You do not need to place 10 trades a day to make a lot of money — I take one trade a day and I call it good" [10:04–10:10]. Focus on the skill first; money comes later [10:14–10:24]. It took him **two years to turn profitable** [09:48]; everyone's timeline differs. Don't trade with friends — different biases contaminate your read [10:07–10:11]. Trading is individualized/one-on-one you vs. the market [09:58].

**Homework — 30-minute meditation.** Dark room, phone outside the door on a 30-minute *alarm* (not a stopwatch), eyes shut, can't stand up, focus on breathing [11:59–12:43]. Purpose: expose how short your attention span is and reframe how long 30 minutes actually is [13:07–13:20]. He used to do 10 min every morning after a cold shower [14:37–14:53].

## Codex interpretation (inference toward machine rules — NOT TJR's words)

- The checklist [03:36–03:49] is the same MSS/FVG entry model implied elsewhere in the course: **liquidity sweep → break of structure (MSS) → wait for FVG fill → confirmation reaction → entry**. For the engine this is an ordered gate, not a scoring blend: ALL four must occur in sequence or the trade is rejected. This lesson does not define the timeframe for each step — that must come from a technical lesson.
- "One trade a day" [10:04] reads as a **soft max-trades-per-day = 1** guardrail for the beginner cohort. TJR states it as his personal cadence + advice, not a hard course rule, so treat as a proposed configurable cap.
- "Don't trade during news" [06:15] aligns with a **news blackout guardrail**, but TJR gives no window, no impact tier, and no instrument list here — thresholds must be sourced from a dedicated news lesson before coding.
- "Wait an extra 1–2 candlesticks for volume/volatility" [08:18] implies a **confirmation-candle requirement** on the entry timeframe, but the count is soft ("one or two") and the timeframe is unspecified.
- The meditation homework is a human self-development exercise with no machine-executable rule.
