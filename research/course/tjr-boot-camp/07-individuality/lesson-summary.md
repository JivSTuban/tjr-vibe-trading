# Lesson 07 — Individuality

## What TJR teaches

Day 7 is a **psychology** lesson (the course alternates psychology and trading days; yesterday was break of structure, tomorrow is liquidity) [00:14, 01:12-01:18]. The theme is **individuality**: "how you guys should not aspire to be somebody else other than you" because "everyone's brain is different… everyone is their own person and you shouldn't try and copy and paste yourself to make you something you are not" [01:21-01:47]. He splits the lesson into a **trading portion first**, then an overarching life portion [02:49-03:04].

**Don't force a strategy that doesn't click.** If TJR is your favorite mentor but his strategy "doesn't click in your head," and another mentor's strategy "you understand completely and it works for you" — "stop trying to force something that doesn't work just because you have some sort of attachment" to it [03:11-03:52]. Being able to admit "I'm not good at this / this is something I am bad at" and make a change is hard but necessary [03:56-04:11]. If you've put in the time trying to learn his strategy and still can't understand it, "that's completely fine, don't listen to me anymore" for strategy — but keep using his **discipline and psychology** content [04:14-04:32].

**Mix strategies into your own.** Take one strategy of his and one you created yourself, or one of his and one from another mentor, and "twist them together to make it your own" [04:33-04:51]. His strategies are "just based off of simple concepts, plugging them into an execution order, and then taking the trade" [05:00-05:10]. As you learn liquidity sweeps, break of structure, fair value gaps, order blocks — you don't need all of them. If you understand only **liquidity + break of structure**, "those two in itself you can take a trade off of"; likewise **order blocks + break of structure + SMT divergence** is a valid combo [05:10-05:33]. (Aside: he says he does NOT trade SMT divergence and doesn't know what it is [05:33-05:43].) "Do what works for you"; if liquidity sweeps never clicked, "don't use them" [05:45-06:05].

**There are a million ways to trade.** Don't let anyone tell you "this is the only way to trade" — "there's a million ways to trade and a bunch of them are profitable" [06:13-06:25]. Don't treat this course/strategy as "locked down" where you must follow every single thing "or else you will suck" — that's not how it works; he's "just teaching you what I know" [06:27-06:43]. If some of his stuff doesn't work, learn from someone else and take bits and pieces [06:45-06:52].

**Origin story (why individuality matters).** His **Forex strategy** — which he's "been killing" — he "made up on my own." He noticed through repetition, "sitting there every London session on GJ [GBPJPY]," a fake-out he named the **"London session fake out"**: right when the London session opens price goes strongly in one direction then immediately flips [06:52-07:27]. That observation "was pretty much my introduction to liquidity sweeps" [07:29-07:31]. Lesson: be your own person, be **adaptable** to yourself and your own needs, not "oh TJR said this so this is law" [07:33-08:24].

**Don't compare to peers.** "This is a race between yourself… don't do this together with your buddies… don't compete with your friends because everyone's learning curve is different" [08:57-09:16]. A friend hitting a second profitable month while you haven't had a profitable week "is going to mess up your psychology" — everyone learns differently [09:16-09:38].

**Life portion.** Don't aspire to literally copy his life (Puerto Rico, a dog like Boogie) — "do what you want with your own life"; don't alter what you wear or your opinions to satisfy others [09:55-10:46]. **Homework:** for one day, be completely yourself — wear an outfit you love, voice your honest opinion, do what you want and "not listening to anybody else for one single day" [10:50-11:07, 15:53-16:08]. Confidence in life is **directly correlated** to confidence in trading: "if you are not confident with yourself… you won't be able to do trading" [13:24-14:05, 14:05-15:00]. Framing: millions go long while millions go short at the same time; you must be confident in your own decision [14:44-14:56]. Closing homework restated: go be confident, take a risk today, be productive, **document how it made you feel** being confident [16:59-17:56, 17:41-17:50].

## Codex interpretation

- This is a **psychology / meta-strategy** lesson. It produces behavioral guardrails, not chart mechanics. The core testable idea: a trader should trade ONLY setups they personally understand and (implicitly) validate, and may drop any component of the taught strategy that "doesn't click."
- Concrete, machine-relevant claims worth flagging: (1) **modular strategy** — the strategy is decomposable into concepts (liquidity sweep, BOS, FVG, order block) plugged into an "execution order"; a valid strategy can use a SUBSET (e.g. liquidity + BOS, or order block + BOS + SMT) rather than all components [05:10-05:33]. (2) TJR **does not trade SMT divergence** [05:33-05:43] — relevant if any downstream rule set assumes SMT. (3) His Forex edge is the **London-session fake-out on GBPJPY (GJ)** — a session-scoped liquidity-sweep reversal; concrete instrument (GJ) + session (London open) are stated [06:52-07:27].
- "GJ" = GBPJPY (ASR abbreviation, consistent with the series where GU=GBPUSD). "SMT Divergence" = Smart Money Technique divergence (correlated-pairs divergence) — TJR explicitly disclaims knowledge/use of it.
- No numbers given except the rhetorical "$50k average American paycheck" [16:00-16:06], which is color, not a trading parameter. No lot size, RR, SL/TP, or session times specified.
- The "don't compete with friends / don't compare learning curves / document how you feel" items convert cleanly into behavioral guardrails for a discretionary-trader-facing system; they are NOT auto-trading signals.
- Homework ("be yourself for one day," "document how you felt") is a journaling directive, not a market rule.
