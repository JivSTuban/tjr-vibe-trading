# Lesson 10 — Liquidity Pt 2 (How to spot it / where it lies)

## What TJR teaches

Part 2 of the three-part liquidity thread. Goal: **how to spot liquidity in the market and where it lies** [00:11-00:28]. How-to-trade is still deferred to Pt 3 (two days later) [00:44, 13:55].

**Recap of Pt 1** [01:15-01:52]: we use liquidity because that's where orders get filled and where banks push the market; banks need people exiting AND people entering the opposite direction (it's an exchange — to fill millions of orders they need millions of opposing orders).

**Where liquidity lies — the core teaching.** Grounded in trend structure (higher highs/higher lows in uptrend; lower highs/lower lows in downtrend) [02:14-02:22]. Retail traders see higher-high/higher-low and assume trend continuation, so:

- **Above highs (in an uptrend):** there are **buy stops** (pending breakout orders to go long on continuation) AND **stop losses** of shorts (who get invalidated if price pushes above the high) [02:47-03:17]. So when a high is taken out, both longs enter and short-stops trigger → liquidity is created for banks to fill orders and reverse (usually to the downside) [03:53-04:10, 05:08-05:36].
- **Below lows (in a downtrend):** mirror image — **sell stops / sell limits** (continuation shorts) AND **stop losses** of longs trying to catch a bounce sit below the low [05:38-06:46]. When the low is taken out, shorts enter + long-stops trigger → banks fill orders and reverse (usually up) [06:48-06:58].

**The liquidation mechanic** [04:14-04:42]: when the high is pushed above, breakout buyers get filled, then their stops (just under the high) get taken out by "a couple pips" and the market moves against them. "They literally liquidated all of those people." This is why trading retail breakout/continuation logic loses — TJR attributes retail success to experience, not method [04:46-05:00]. Because trends don't last forever, playing liquidity lets you catch the top of a downtrend / bottom of an uptrend by entering at the *beginning* of the new trend [05:54-06:14].

**The order block aside** [06:56-07:15]: the down-move that causes the liquidation and lets the market fill orders — "this whole thing would essentially be an order block" (deferred for later).

**Chart demonstration** [07:19 onward]: the sweep pattern is "very very easy... jumps off the page" and happens on **every single timeframe**. Examples cited: uptrend high swept → downtrend; S&P 500 daily highs swept → big sell-off (multiple); a monthly low sweep visible on the yearly timeframe = "a massive liquidity sweep," expected reversal, coinciding with a weak US dollar [08:52-09:10]; Asian session mini-downtrend → BOS down / take lows / rally / BOS up; 15-minute, weekly, monthly examples; noting the current monthly candle sits at a big high with 28 days left to close, guessing a downside sweep [13:23-13:54]. Repeated chant: "sweep → fall" / "sweep → rally."

**Attribution** [15:15-15:22]: "this is how ICT literally came up with this."

## Codex interpretation

- **Liquidity-detection heuristic (machine-codable):** Liquidity pools sit immediately ABOVE prior swing highs and BELOW prior swing lows. In an uptrend, target buy-side liquidity above highs; in a downtrend, target sell-side liquidity below lows. Prominence of the swing (Pt 3 refines this) matters.
- **Sweep = price trades beyond a prior swing extreme then reverses.** TJR's "couple of pips" beyond the high implies the sweep can be shallow (wick/marginal penetration) — exact threshold still undefined.
- The **reversal expectation after a sweep is the tradeable edge**, but Pt 1's rule still governs: NO entry without subsequent MSS/BOS confirmation. Pt 2 adds *location* (above highs / below lows) to Pt 1's *mechanism*.
- "Order block" is introduced as the origin candle/zone of the impulsive move that did the liquidating — flagged for a later dedicated lesson.
- ICT lineage = provenance note, not a rule.
