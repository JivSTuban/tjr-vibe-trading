# Lesson 01 — Take Action

## What TJR teaches

This is the boot-camp **intro / "contract"** video, explicitly a psychology lesson, not a strategy lesson. TJR opens with his signature greeting ("what is good jits", ASR "jit lips") from Puerto Rico and states up front: **"this video you aren't going to learn anything"** — it is "our little contract between me and you" [00:47-00:53]. He frames the whole boot camp as **Lesson 1: Take Action** [04:21-04:30, 04:45].

**The contract (two-way agreement).** TJR promises to upload **one video every single day** [03:20-03:24, 20:38-20:44] and to give away his full method for free. In return he asks for **compliance**: watch every video, do every piece of homework, use *everything* (not "bits and pieces"), and rewatch lessons multiple times to understand [01:17-01:56]. **If you miss a day, go back and watch the missed video AND that day's video** [03:24-03:32]. This is meant to be watched **start to finish** — not "a couple of videos" [03:08-03:19]. If you don't understand something, **ask in the comments** [02:50-02:55]. He warns he will **stop the series halfway** if viewers treat it like a joke / retention is low [21:43-21:47, 20:38-20:56]. He asks viewers to comment "I'm down / I'm for it / I'll do it" as their side of the contract [21:37-21:46].

**Reset your expectations (get-rich-quick).** TJR says he too wanted to get rich fast. Reality: **you will NOT get rich in a day, a week, a month, two months, or three months** — forget that [05:23-05:42]. Realistic timeline: **potentially/likely rich in one to two years, and that IS quick** [05:47-05:57]. "This shit took years" [11:19-11:22].

**Forget everything / blank slate.** Step one: **forget every little thing you ever thought you knew about trading** [09:35-09:48]. Don't jump ahead asking about fair value / equilibrium — "we'll get to it" — he'll explain at a "first-grade / third-grade comprehension level" [07:51-08:11]. Stop over-analyzing; be **real with yourself, admit when you're wrong**, and understand it was *your* misread of the market, not the strategy's fault [09:19-09:51].

**His origin story (why market experience matters).** He fell for the "100% win rate / 1:3 every time" lie videos [06:30-06:58], signed up to ~10 signal groups and blew his account [10:11-10:21], bought **3 courses (~$2,000)** that were all garbage [10:22-10:33], got **scammed out of ~$5,000** [11:35-11:42]. What finally worked: **he removed every indicator, stripped the charts, and just watched the market** — that's where he saw the most growth [12:16-12:40]. He explicitly says he **did NOT learn from ICT** — he discovered liquidity sweeps himself and called them a **"London fake out"**: trading **GJ (GBPJPY)**, at London session open price would fake out then reverse; when it **took out a high he'd press sell** with a **15-pip stop loss** [37:20-37:52]. He only heard of ICT after already being profitable, and gives ICT respect as a better trader [38:01-38:34].

**No shortcut / put in the reps.** He'll give "every piece of information you need but that's not enough" — **you must put time in on the charts** [14:11-14:37]. Basketball-highlights analogy: watching dunks won't let you recreate them without practice [12:52-13:12]. **Delete a social app** (TikTok/Instagram/Snapchat) if it eats an hour, and reclaim that hour — "this is going to take an hour out of your day" [17:17-17:31, 16:56-17:03]. Put yourself in a **position with zero room for failure** (he was in ~$2,000 of bank debt, DoorDashing, depressed, "rock bottom" ~3.5-4 years ago) — desperation forced the discipline [22:25-25:00].

**Journaling rule.** He turns journaling into a **rule**: "let's make it a fucking rule — journal, note this." He commits to journal his own process while making the course; viewers journal their market experience [40:29-40:56].

**Homework.** He states some homework **won't even relate to trading** — a lot of it is mental/mindset work [41:51-42:07]. No single concrete chart task is assigned in *this* video; the recurring ask is: watch daily, do the homework, journal, take action, be different, delete the time-wasting app.

**Closing.** He becomes emotional recounting depression/bullying (by his brother) and being fat as a kid [47:04-49:06], says he's ~27,000 subscribers at recording [49:50], motto **"we can do all things"** [51:34-51:51], and signs off "I'll see you boys tomorrow… on the charts."

## Codex interpretation

- This lesson yields **behavioral / discipline guardrails**, not chart rules. The only near-mechanical items: (1) daily-cadence + catch-up rule, (2) mandatory journaling, (3) delete a time-sink app to reclaim ≥1 hr/day, (4) reset profitability horizon to ~1-2 years.
- The **"London fake out" anecdote is the one proto-strategy nugget**: instrument GJ/GBPJPY, timeframe implicitly intraday at **London session open**, mechanic = fade the liquidity sweep of a session high/low with a **15-pip fixed SL**. This is presented as historical backstory, NOT as the taught system, so it is captured as an example with `suitable_for_dataset: false` and flagged for confirmation — later lessons (esp. 08 Liquidity) formalize sweeps/MSS.
- Numbers to treat as **color, not parameters**: $2,000 courses, $5,000 scam, ~$2,000 debt, $25k/$30k-per-trade, "27,000 subs", 2M-dollar house, "10x", house/watch/silver-bar flexes. None are risk parameters.
- The "stop the series if retention is low / comment to opt in" and "98%…"-style rhetoric are engagement framing, not system rules.
- Cross-lesson hook: liquidity-sweep + "took out a high → sell" here is the seed that Lesson 08 (Liquidity Pt 1) develops into the formal sweep → MSS/BOS confirmation chain. The 15-pip SL is a legacy hardcode, almost certainly superseded later.
