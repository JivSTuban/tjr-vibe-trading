# Lesson 08 — Liquidity Pt 1

## What TJR teaches

TJR opens the liquidity series, calling liquidity "the largest topic and the most important topic of my strategy" [00:23]. This is Part 1 of a planned three-part series; Part 1 covers only **what liquidity is and why we use it / how it makes the market move** — explicitly NOT how to spot it or how to trade it (those come in Parts 2 and 3) [00:52-01:10].

**Definition.** Liquidity is what TJR "would like to call resting orders" [02:30]. Multiple order types count as liquidity: stop orders (getting people out of a trade) and limit orders (buy limit / sell limit) [02:39-02:48]. When these price targets get hit, something activates — it either takes you out of the market or enters you into the market [02:53-02:56]. He also frames it as "a pool of money" — a price point where, when achieved, people enter and exit, meaning a lot of money gets cycled through the market at that moment [02:58-03:15].

**Why banks seek it.** Banks / institutions / "Market Movers" have massive orders [03:53-04:04]. Every trade requires a counterparty — to buy you need someone to sell (stock-exchange analogy: buying a share of Apple requires a seller) [04:06-04:47]. Aside: some offshore brokerages "book" the trader — they trade against you / fill your order themselves, "assuming that you are going to lose the trade because 98% of traders fail" [05:00-05:20]. Banks fill their massive orders **through liquidity sweeps / taking out liquidity** [05:52-05:57] — they need many people exiting and entering the opposite direction so their orders fill and they can push the market whichever way they want [06:28-06:38].

**Why we care / how we act.** We want to trade in the same direction as the market movers [06:47-06:52]. The play: understand where liquidity is → wait for liquidity to be taken out → then get a **confirmation**, specifically a **break of structure / market structure shift** (trend shift / change in direction) [07:05-07:20]. The MSS confirms "liquidity has been swept and orders have been filled causing the market to change direction" [07:30-07:36]. That is what makes trades hit take-profit — NOT "it bounced off a floor" or "hit its head on the ceiling" (he repeatedly mocks support/resistance as guessing) [01:25-01:52, 07:39-07:47].

**Rule of restraint.** It is easy to mark liquidity everywhere; but if price reaches it you do NOT immediately press buy/sell — "we need more confirmation" [08:47-08:57]. If you don't see a shift in direction, "hands off, we're not doing anything" [08:40-08:44]. Entry is timed AFTER the sweep + MSS; we don't execute exactly when the banks do — "we got to wait a little bit" because we don't know the exact moment they press the button, but we still get an incredibly good entry, "literally catching the tops and the bottoms of moves" (his trades are at the top/bottom of a trend, not mid-trend) [10:03-10:26].

**Homework.** No real homework — just rewatch this video 3–4 times and fully understand the concept of liquidity [11:21-11:37].

## Codex interpretation

- Liquidity = clusters of resting orders (pending stop orders + limit orders) at price levels, typically above swing highs and below swing lows (specific location NOT given here — deferred to Pt 2). Treat as a to-be-confirmed pool, not a signal by itself.
- The tradeable event chain implied: (1) price sweeps a liquidity level → (2) MSS/BOS in the opposite direction confirms institutional order fill → (3) enter in the new direction. This is a confirmation-gated reversal pattern, NOT a level-touch entry. All three stages are load-bearing; absence of stage 2 = no trade.
- "98% of traders fail" and "book the trader" are context/color, not rules.
- Concrete thresholds (which highs/lows qualify, how much of a sweep, which timeframe MSS) are all UNSPECIFIED in this lesson.
