# Lesson 45 — Over Complicating

## What TJR teaches

A pure mindset lesson (opens with a comedic mitochondria tangent) whose one message
is: **stop over-complicating trading — the missing ingredient is time in the market,
not more strategy.**

**Trust the trading plan** [03:39–04:16]. "That's why we made our trading plan … so we literally have no other way to mess up besides the market going against us. We know how many times we're trading, when we're trading, why we're trading, how to execute — we're following a step-by-step plan." Follow the plan day in and day out and you'll turn profitable over time.

**The missing link is time / market experience, not a fix** [04:26–06:14, 10:53–12:19]. When results are slow, students wrongly stray from the plan thinking it doesn't work; in reality "you just have no market experience." "We have the same strategy … it's your ability to read price action." "There's no missing link — the missing link is just time." He estimates **~1 year minimum** for the average disciplined person to turn profitable [11:33]. Understanding daily bias (where price wants to go on the day) is "pretty easy"; the *hard* part is **execution** — finding the right areas to execute and getting good risk-reward, which only improves through repetition [05:57–06:19].

**One strategy, one bias — stop stacking** [08:35–12:38]. He simplifies smart-money concepts hard. Having ~20 strategies / 20 biases running at once is the problem; "clear the search history in your brain," use one strategy, find one bias, and **stop listening to other traders / signal groups / your favorite YouTuber** (including his own daily bias once you can find your own). Anecdote: when he still followed a signal group, sometimes his own read was right and the mentor wrong ("you trade the same way and see something different that day"), and he lost by taking the mentor's bias over his own [10:16–10:52]. Goal is to graduate from needing the Discord (he says he expects members "out of here in ~3 months") [13:17–13:33].

**Don't add — learn from what you have** [16:05–19:36]. "It's not what can I add to this to make it better, it's what can I learn from this to make it better." Adding a component means you must become an *expert* at that component too — each addition adds time to profitability. Instead be an expert at the **four building blocks** (spotting fair value gaps, order blocks, liquidity sweeps, breaks of structure) and at finding daily bias with the one strategy. Analogy: Allen Iverson had one killer crossover everyone saw coming and still burned them — "if it ain't broke don't fix it."

**Journal to encode experience** [14:01–15:47, 20:36–21:26]. Write down why you did/didn't take a trade and what the market then did ("I was right because…"/"I was wrong, I should have accounted for this"); the same setups recur ~100 times, and writing it down makes you remember the lesson next time. He journaled ~2 years; his journal is now his YouTube trade recaps (previously long Discord write-ups). "Get the [screen] in front of a chart and literally watch the market move and study it — become an expert at how it reacts off certain price points."

## Codex interpretation

- This is a **process/discipline** lesson; its machine value is guardrails, not signals.
- **Single-strategy invariant**: the system should implement exactly one strategy with one bias output per instrument — no ensemble of conflicting sub-strategies, no external signal ingestion. This is an architectural constraint TJR states explicitly.
- **Don't-add invariant**: freeze the feature set at the four building blocks (FVG, OB, liquidity sweep, BOS) + daily bias; improvement should come from parameter tuning / experience data, not new indicators.
- **Experience ~ time**: sets realistic expectation (≈1 year to profitability). Not a machine rule, but a note against over-fitting to short evaluation windows.
- **Journaling loop** (shared with 44 & 46): log rationale + outcome for recurring-setup recognition; supports a persistent taste/pattern memory.
