# Lesson 11 — How to Take a Loss

## What TJR teaches

A discipline video positioned as "the most important lesson" as the course starts merging into how to take trades [00:06-00:16]. No charts.

**Core mindset.** Realize that losing is part of the game — you WILL lose, and you should *encourage* it because it means you're learning [00:49-01:00]. "No profitable trader ever has a 100% win rate that trades consistently... they always lose at least once. The market is built to beat you" [02:42-02:54]. Getting upset / throwing a fit is "literally the opposite of what you should be doing" — it's letting emotions take over and usually signals you over-risked or took too many trades [02:56-03:22].

**Learn from the loss, not blame.** After a loss, don't ask "why did I take this trade?" — ask "why didn't it go in my direction?" and "how can I learn from this?" [04:09-04:16, 07:52-08:15]. It's all probability: the same exact setup could win tomorrow [04:44-04:57]. The market has "mixed personalities" / different conditions every day — "it's an algorithm" [05:03-05:27].

**Demo first.** Encourage losing *on demo* — you'll be trading demo anyway, so lose there and save real money + emotional trauma [03:53-04:01, 10:00-11:26]. Path: get profitable on demo → move to live → expect ~a week of emotional losses → then "slap yourself in the face," trust your proven demo edge, and follow the plan. "It'll be like a one-month turnaround" [10:26-11:33].

**Be robotic.** "Be robotic with this... you are the algo, you're the EA, you're the trading bot, treat yourself like that" [11:59-12:09]. Follow the trading plan step by step (plan-building covered later in the boot camp) [11:53-11:58].

**No revenge trading.** "When I lose I don't revenge trade, I don't over trade, I don't react — there should be no reaction besides happiness" [12:28-12:47]. He cites losing **$9,000** on a GU trade and **$10,000** and still smiling / living his best life, because he stuck to his risk rules and knows the money comes back [01:18-01:24, 13:00-13:22]. Old self was "a rage monster" who screamed into a pillow — caused by trying to get rich quick [09:22-09:42].

**Take days off when edge is low.** Take days off for news, being up late, or emotional states (e.g., a breakup) where you're least probable to win and most likely to react [13:43-14:24].

**Minimize the reaction.** You WILL get upset without a doubt; the skill is taking a step back in that moment, recognizing "this was me overreacting / I'm doing something I shouldn't," making the reaction very minimal and short-lived [14:29-15:15].

**Think in yearly %.** "Stop thinking day to day / week to week / month to month — what's coming home in the year? What percentage gain am I getting?" Once you can take a loss + know your strategy + treat it as a yearly percentage game, the only remaining constraint is capital [15:52-16:41].

**Ownership.** "It's not the market's fault, it's your fault... you took the wrong trade." Even if news stopped you out, the lesson is "maybe I shouldn't trade before news" [16:54-17:33].

## Codex interpretation

Discipline lesson → behavioral guardrails for the risk engine / execution layer:
- **No revenge/over-trading after a loss** — enforce a per-loss cooldown; do not re-enter to "win it back."
- **No-trade filters when edge is degraded** — block trading around high-impact news, after insufficient sleep, or flagged emotional states.
- **Demo-gate before live** — require a demonstrated profitable demo track record before enabling live sizing.
- **Deterministic execution** — "be the algo": follow a fixed rule set; loss outcome must not alter subsequent decisions.
- **Long-horizon (annual %) performance metric** — evaluate the system yearly, not per-session (reinforces lesson 09/13).
Dollar figures ($9k, $10k losses; 112k flip in lesson 11 recap) are personal anecdotes — motivational, not parameters.
