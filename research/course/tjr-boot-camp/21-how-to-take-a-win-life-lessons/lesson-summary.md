## Layer 1 — What TJR teaches (faithful to transcript)

### Framing: a win is more dangerous than a loss
TJR opens saying "how to take a win" is *more important* than how to take a loss [00:25], because a win breeds overconfidence, greed, overleveraging, and overtrading — "winning accidentally and then getting too much confidence and spiraling out of control" [01:39].

### Treat a win exactly like a loss — be emotionless
"When you win you should treat it no different than a loss… you should be emotionless" [02:03]. He claims to be emotionless whether he wins $20,000 or loses $14,000 the same morning [02:08]–[02:20] (ASR renders it "$144,000" but he clarifies the loss was **$14,000** at [10:40]). Framing: a result is "just a percentage gain, that's a probability" [02:13].

### Think in probabilities/percentages, not cash
"The most important part about all of this is thinking in probabilities" [02:48]. If you think purely about "cold hard cash you're getting fucked… you cannot act based off money" [02:55]–[03:46]. He illustrates: a **$10M account**, risking **1% = $100k**; a **1:1 RR** win = $100k [03:03]–[03:24]. Most people, seeing price reach ~**50k** (half of the 1R target), panic-close "close close close I'm securing all that bread" — but "it didn't hit your take profit, it didn't hit your stop loss, why are you acting on emotion?" [03:34]–[03:43]. **Do not close early on emotion; let it hit TP or SL.**

### Personal example — hold to the plan
Three months prior, when he upped his lot sizes, he had days with **$330,000 floating profit with his stop loss at break even** that he "could have closed and didn't, because I had to stick to my plan" [04:03]–[04:14]. Separately, ~a month and a half ago he **made 60 bands (~$60k) in a day**, "could have made 100k that day," the rest of the position **got stopped out at break even** — and "guess what, I stuck to my trading plan" [20:43]–[21:05]. Message: sticking to the plan matters more than the maximized dollar outcome.

### One trade a day → then walk away
"This is why just taking one trade a day is so beneficial… take one trade and just go home… whether it's a win or a loss you're done, you're done for the day" [04:59]–[05:08]. Rationale: taking another trade after a win because "I just made this much, let me risk more so I can make more" is overconfidence [04:41]–[04:57]; wanting to keep going is *greed*, not wanting to give profits back is *fear* [05:12]–[05:23]. "I took my trade and I'm done, whether it's a win or a loss, I don't give a fuck, and I'm just done" [05:23].

### Do not celebrate / do not screenshot & post
"Should you be happy about a win? No. You've got to be emotionless" [05:32]. If you screenshot a running trade, "you should probably close that trade" [06:07] — many of his screenshotted running trades marked the exact top before price came back and stopped him out [06:17]–[06:30]. Posting P&L, celebrating, telling people "I just made 100K" is "emotions talking" and an issue to cut immediately [05:45]–[06:03]. "Act like you've been there before" [20:29]. Jumping around celebrating a win tells him "you have never made bread in this before" [20:34]–[20:40]. He's fine with being *proud* but "don't be satisfied… because you're still not profitable" [09:34]–[09:41].

### A win doesn't matter day-to-day — zoom out to month/year
"A win don't matter until the end of the year and you see your win rate… end of the month and you see if you're up or down" [11:15]–[11:26]. "Stop focusing on the damn day to day" [11:30]. Concretely: **stop watching the account balance go up and down; take the trade on TradingView and forget about it, don't even look at the account balance** [11:39]–[12:09]. Check the MetaTrader P&L at end of month / end of year, not daily [13:19]–[13:32].

### A win is a learning opportunity — journal it, find why you won
"A loss you're paying for a lesson; a win, cool" [10:58]. Don't just celebrate — **analyze why you won and figure out how to repeat it** [13:39]–[15:44]: "half the time y'all are winning and getting lucky, y'all don't even know why you won" [14:08]. Journal both wins and losses; his old TikTok trade recaps *were* his trading journal [17:36]–[18:03]. A win is "more rare than a loss so take advantage" [17:00]. Goal is to **improve 1% better every day** [18:49]–[18:53]; a win is "definitely not a day off," you should be "hungry for more" — meaning hungry to repeat the process, *not* to trade more [18:57]–[19:08].

### The template of a clean win (his self-review checklist) [19:41]–[20:25]
Followed bias → checked weekly TF → checked daily TF → all aligned with bias → scaled to lower TF → all three biases aligned → looked for longs → equilibrium + FVG + order block present → price moved into it → saw MSS → saw BOS → entered → set TPs off previous liquidity areas → managed positions → when TPs hit, moved SL to break even. "Today was good — that is what you need to be doing."

### Core creed
"We are here to learn the skill of trading, not the money from trading… if you have this skill, money will come regardless" [14:41]–[15:03]. Two losing days can wipe out huge gains, so a win is fragile [10:58]–[11:00].

## Layer 2 — Codex interpretation (inference toward machine rules — NOT TJR's words)

- **Win-then-stop is the cleanest testable guardrail:** after the first trade of the session closes (win OR loss), lock the account out of new entries for the rest of that session/day. TJR states "one trade a day" and "done for the day" explicitly; the machine form is a hard trade-count cap of 1/day.
- **No emotional early exit:** the engine should only close on TP or SL, never a discretionary partial before TP. TJR's "let it hit TP or SL" implies disabling manual mid-trade closes (except the stated break-even SL trail after a TP is hit).
- **Break-even trail after first TP:** the win-template explicitly says "when take profits were hit I moved my stop loss to break even" — this is a concrete, codeable position-management rule, though thresholds (which TP, how many TPs) are undefined.
- **Metric to optimize = win rate / % return over month & year, not daily P&L.** A scorer should evaluate on monthly/annual aggregates, not per-day.
- **Mandatory post-win journaling / attribution** could be enforced as a required log entry before the next session unlocks.
- Numbers like "$10M / 1% / $100k / 1:1" are *illustrative examples*, not prescribed account size or RR. "50k" is just half of that example's 1R (an early-close temptation), not a rule. Do not hard-code these.
