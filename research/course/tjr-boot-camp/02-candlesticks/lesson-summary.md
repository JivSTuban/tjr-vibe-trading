# Lesson 02 — Candlesticks

## What TJR teaches

Day 2 is framed as a "from scratch" foundational lesson — TJR warns experienced viewers "you probably won't learn much" but promises to toss in beginner tips along the way [00:12-00:35]. Topic: candlesticks, price movement, and understanding price movement within candlesticks [00:37-00:44]. He is on the S&P 500 chart and notes essentially every chart on any ticker symbol displays candlesticks [00:44-01:09].

**Candlesticks describe price.** Candlesticks "describe price" through **bodies and Wicks** [01:11-01:17]. They are called candlesticks "because they leave Wicks," and each candlestick "tells a story" [01:19-01:24]. He draws two candles by hand: one **down candle** (bearish) and one **up candle** (bullish) [02:24-02:45].

**Timeframe = time to close.** Before reading candles you must understand what timeframe you are on. The many timeframes (1 min, 2 min, 3 min, 5 min, 7 min, 8 min, etc.) each represent "the time that it takes for the candlestick to close" [03:04-03:26]. On the 5-minute chart (which he is on), one candle represents five minutes of price action / price movement / market movement [03:26-03:45, 04:23-04:32]. The value: with just one candlestick you can read exactly what price did within those five minutes; TJR claims that with experience he can even guess one-minute structure off a single five-minute candle or two [03:48-04:22].

**Candle anatomy — bullish (green) candle.** Green means price went up; a green/bullish candle "goes up and ends right up here" [04:32-04:41]. On a green candle: price **opened** where the body of the candle starts (the bottom of the body) and **closed** at the five-minute mark at the top of the body [04:49-05:10]. He labels the top of the body as the close and the bottom as the open [05:05-05:25].

**Wicks.** The little Wicks left behind show "where price went during that five minute duration of time" but never ended up closing there — "it just shows where price went," a "little snail Trail" [05:29-05:45]. An upper wick means within that five-minute candle price moved up to that point but did not close there [06:14-06:24]. The top of the upper wick = the **highest price point** price reached within those five minutes; the bottom of the lower wick = the **lowest price point** price reached [06:25-06:38].

**Candle anatomy — bearish (red) candle.** Same concept but inverted: red means down. On a bearish candle the **open is up here** (higher, top of body) and the **close is down here** (lower, bottom of body) — "the close on a bearish candle is lower obviously and the open on a bearish candle is higher" [06:42-07:07]. The top of the wick is still the highest point price reached and the bottom is the lowest point price reached [07:08-07:19]. He summarizes: "candlesticks simplified by TJR" [07:19-07:24].

**Candlestick patterns — what he keeps vs rejects.** TJR states he hates candlestick patterns based on "mountain tops," W's, and Head & Shoulders — "that shit is BS" — and he hates every candlestick pattern that contains more than two candles [07:42-08:09]. The only patterns he likes, because "they can actually show you price action and dictate where price is going to go," are **single candlestick patterns** [08:09-08:21]. He pulls a "candlestick patterns cheat sheet" and says he only cares about one row: **doji's, long-legged doji's (which are the same thing as doji's), cross doji's, inverse cross doji's, dragonfly doji's, and hammers** — "forget about all this" other stuff [09:01-09:22]. Reason: these candlesticks show price action and show where price rejected and where it didn't [09:33-09:44].

**Reading a rejection candle (upper wick).** For a candle that opens, pushes all the way up but then closes back down leaving a "fat ass Wick up here": it says "we have strong bulls but not strong enough to close" all the way up. It closed bullish but showed weakness because it couldn't finish out the candle; the long upper wick means it was not strong enough to close above whatever was residing above, and "more than likely it was taking this liquidity" [09:50-10:53].

**Doji = indecision.** Doji's are "just small bodies of candles" — small body, long wicks, or just a small body in general [11:05-11:28]. Doji's are great for reading price action; they show **indecision in the market**, and "indecision with a bias in one direction" [11:16-11:49]. You do NOT take a trade off a doji alone, but you must be able to read what it's saying [11:49-12:00]. He contrasts good traders (who read candles) with people who just say "liquidity was swept, we broke structure" but can't even read the candlesticks [12:02-12:13]. Example: a big sell-off then a candle with long wicks to the downside but no full body close means "we're losing bearish momentum" — and indeed price then consolidated and pulled up [12:15-12:43]. A long upper wick failing to close above a price point, followed by a fall, is the same idea [13:01-13:24].

**Don't trade off patterns alone (Gold daily example).** On Gold daily he shows a bullish doji: strong rejection to the downside (long lower wick) with a bullish close, but price was in a downtrend continuously breaking structure to the downside. "This is why we cannot be taking trades purely off of candlestick patterns because this is only one confluence" — don't let your bias get lost in these patterns [14:00-15:03].

**Strong close vs weak close.** A "strong close" is what a regular full-bodied candlestick looks like — full body, not much wick against the direction of the close [15:14-15:29]. A bearish example: a downside move with a small wick to the downside relative to the body, able to close and continue down — strong — versus a wick to the downside that then moves back up, which shows weakness [15:37-15:56].

**Confirmation candle / sweep example (Gold weekly-ish).** A candle with a long lower wick that swept liquidity and then closed strongly bullish acts "almost like a confirmation candle." Key discipline: understand price opened where it opened; at one point during the period it was a big red candle. People "get so caught up in the candle that's forming and not the candle that has formed." "We can only make bias and decision on candles that have formed, not candles that are forming" — "that's why we always wait for a close, for a break of structure." A candle is not an actual candle until price action is done within the timeframe [16:10-17:24]. The strong bullish example: opened, pushed all the way down (massive lower wick), rallied back and closed near the highs (tiny upper wick) = "a very very strong bullish candle" that tells you exactly where price wants to go; price then rallied and took out the highs [17:27-18:29].

**Homework.** Get on whatever pair/chart you trade (he mentions SPX, GU = GBP/USD, GJ = GBP/JPY) and **find at least 10 examples** of these doji candlesticks / wick-rejection candles, take screenshots, label them, and study **how price ended up reacting off of these examples** [18:59-20:41, 22:00-22:30]. He cites the saying "Wicks don't lie" (maybe by a YouTube/Instagram teacher, or maybe it's "fibs don't lie" — he's unsure) — "Wicks and candlesticks tell the whole story of price action" [20:44-21:05]. Closing thesis: "if you cannot understand candlesticks you won't be able to understand the market" [22:44-22:55]. Very short "syllabus week" lesson [23:00-23:18].

## Codex interpretation

- **Candle OHLC anatomy (core, unambiguous):**
  - Body = span between open and close.
  - Bullish (green) candle: open = bottom of body, close = top of body (close > open).
  - Bearish (red) candle: open = top of body, close = bottom of body (close < open).
  - Upper wick top = high (max price in the period). Lower wick bottom = low (min price in the period).
  - Timeframe = the fixed duration a candle aggregates before it closes (5-min chart → each candle = 5 minutes). This is standard OHLC; encode directly, high confidence.
- **Only-formed-candles rule:** decisions may only use CLOSED candles, never the currently-forming candle. This is a hard, testable gating rule (wait for candle close). High confidence.
- **Single-candle patterns only:** TJR's tradeable candle vocabulary is restricted to single-candle forms — doji / long-legged doji / cross doji / inverse cross doji / dragonfly doji / hammer. Multi-candle chart patterns (H&S, double tops/W's, "mountain tops") are explicitly excluded. Note: he treats "long-legged doji" and "doji" as identical here.
- **Doji = indecision, NOT a standalone entry.** A doji signals indecision (optionally with directional bias); it is "one confluence" only. No trade is taken on a candle pattern alone — it must combine with other confluence (liquidity sweep + break of structure, developed in later lessons). Confirmation-gated.
- **Strong vs weak close (qualitative, no numeric threshold):** "strong close" = large body with small opposing wick, closing in the direction of intent and continuing. "Weak close" = large opposing wick / small body / reversal after the wick. TJR gives NO numeric wick:body ratio — thresholds are undefined (see ambiguities). Treat all "strong/weak," "big/fat/small" wick language as qualitative until a threshold is confirmed.
- **Rejection / wick logic:** a long wick against the close direction = price attempted that level, was rejected, and "more than likely" took liquidity there. This links candlesticks to the liquidity thread (lesson 08) but here it is descriptive, not a rule.
- Vulgar analogies ("snail trail," Calvin Klein underwear) are color, not content.
- No trade examples with instrument+direction+outcome+entry/SL/TP are given — the chart walk-throughs are illustrative anatomy/reading exercises, not executable setups (examples.yaml skipped; see completion.json).
