# Lesson 06 — Break of Structure (BOS)

## What TJR teaches

This is Boot Camp Day 6. TJR announces the topic up front: "we are going to be talking about Break of structure" [00:34]. He explicitly sequences it before liquidity: "right after this trading video the next trading video is going to be talking about liquidity" [00:54] — BOS is taught FIRST because it is the confirmation primitive liquidity trades depend on. He builds on prior lessons: candlesticks are known, and trends are known ("now we know Trends" [01:07]); this lesson explains "why and when the Market's trending and when it actually switches Direction when it breaks structure when it gets a market structure shift" [01:13-01:19]. **TJR uses "break of structure," "market structure shift," "trend shift," and "change in direction" interchangeably in this lesson** [01:17-01:24, 00:34].

**Core definition.** "The simplest way that I can describe a break of structure is a shift in the trend" [01:27]. Trends move in higher highs and higher lows (uptrend) or lower lows and lower highs (downtrend) [01:34-01:49]. A BOS is when that structure stops holding: an uptrend "ends up breaking… it's going to start making lower lows and lower highs and that is what we call a break of structure" [00:44-00:59], "meaning we break the trend that the market is currently in… then flip Market Direction / flip Market structure in the other direction" [07:02-07:15]. He stresses it is simple but heavily misunderstood: "for some reason I get lots of questions on it" [07:22].

**Prerequisite: what makes a high / a low (the 2-candle rule).** Before BOS, TJR defines swing points from scratch — something he says he deliberately saved from the Trends lesson [01:52-01:58]. "A high is a move up then a move down; a low is a move down then a move up" [02:15-02:20]. Critically: "it only takes two candles… it's a Candlestick pattern" [02:23-02:28]. One green (up) candle then one red (down) candle = a high; one red (down) candle then one green (up) candle = a low [02:28-02:43]. It does NOT need to look symmetric/pretty [02:43-02:56]. When asked which wick marks the high across the two candles: "you're going to take the highest Wick of whichever of those two candles it is" [04:40-04:45]; for a low, take the lowest wick of the two candles [04:51-05:02]. He validates this by zooming into a 4H candle to show the true low is the extreme wick, not the candle open [05:43-06:12].

**The load-bearing rule: BODY CLOSE, not wick.** This is the anchor claim of the lesson. A break of structure to the UPSIDE (a downtrend flipping up) "is when we have a closure — a candle closure not a wick, a candle closure — above a high" [07:54-08:05]. "We have to see a single candle close fully, meaning closing above this high, meaning the candle body has to be above the wick of the high" [08:12-08:25]. For the downside, the body must close BELOW the most recent low [08:56-09:36, 23:55]. He hammers the negative case: "we do not care about wicks going above highs or going below lows — that does not mean anything to us. We need a candle close" [12:45-12:59]. He commands the viewer to write it down twice: **"it is not a break of structure unless the candle closes above a high or below a low — write that down"** [13:17-13:26].

**Directional watch logic (the checklist).** TJR builds an on-screen step-by-step "formula" [10:07, 14:11, 20:37]:
1. **Identify the trend** — up or down [14:11-14:17, 20:37].
2. **If in an uptrend, watch the LOWS** — "because if we're moving up higher and higher, when are we going to get a break of structure? When the low gets taken out, when the low gets closed below. We don't care about the highs" [14:59-15:17].
3. **If in a downtrend, watch the HIGHS** — the only way for a downtrend to flip up is to close above a high [15:22-15:31, 20:51-21:00].
4. **BOS to the downside** (uptrend → downtrend) = "it has to close below the most recent low" [16:53-16:58, 23:55].
5. **BOS to the upside** (downtrend → uptrend) = it has to close above the most recent high [16:03-16:10, 21:34-21:38].

**Most-recent swing only.** Repeatedly: "I only care about the most recent high or low that has been made" [09:04-09:06], "we don't care about pass lows" [08:23-08:28], "who cares about this low if this new low was already made" [28:06-28:10]. Old swing points are discarded once a newer one forms; only the most recent qualifying swing needs to be closed through to confirm a BOS [27:26-27:35, 31:29-31:34]. He also notes a forming high can still shift: "if the next candle that forms is down, then yeah we'll move the high to there" [21:19-21:26].

**Timeframe / HTF hierarchy.** BOS is timeframe-relative and TJR ties it to the trend-lesson HTF hierarchy: "the weekly holds more power than the daily, the monthly holds more power than the weekly" [22:41-22:47]. Key scenario: "if we see a break of structure to the downside on the daily… but the weekly we're still bullish, it's just a retracement" [22:47-23:18]. He waits for a specific timeframe to close: "we have to wait for the daily time frame to close above here" [21:02-21:08]. So a BOS on a lower timeframe inside a higher-timeframe trend is treated as a retracement, not a true reversal of the dominant bias.

**"This is NOT a BOS" counterexamples (the heart of the lesson).**
- **Wick-through with no body close** [12:11-13:26]: a wick pokes above a prior high (technically a higher high by wick) but the body does not close above — "we don't care because it doesn't close above it, it is not a break of structure." Same for a wick below a low.
- **Liquidity sweep faking a BOS** [23:55-25:00]: a wick below a previous low that does NOT close below "looks like a liquidity sweep… oftentimes market will do this a lot to fake people out who think that a wick below a previous low is actually a break of structure." Market then reverses up. He shows a full black (down) candle where "90% of retailers were just short, it's going down forever," but market "never breaks structure and we push higher" [25:31-25:51]. "The trend never was broken. These were just liquidity sweeps and we will learn about that next time" [26:15-26:25] — an explicit forward-link to Lesson 08 liquidity.
- **Consolidation** [19:43-20:20]: pure alternating highs/lows with no direction is NOT a trend and produces no BOS — "there was no trend within this, there was no break of structure, market had no clue where it wanted to go" (shown on Gold, "a terrible trading week").

**Instruments / timeframes shown.** Gold [20:13, 24:41]; GBPUSD ("GU") on the daily [22:51-22:57]; a "GJ chart" (GBPJPY) reference [27:41]; 4H [05:43], 15-minute [22:07], daily [21:05, 24:41], weekly [21:49, 22:22]. He does not walk numeric prices/RR — examples are structural (drawn), not trade tickets.

**Do NOT over-extrapolate.** "I don't want you to… instantly be like when there's a break of structure it means it's gonna go up forever… that is not true at all" [22:14-22:27]. A BOS confirms a shift; it does not guarantee an indefinite run.

**Homework.** Find 10 real breaks of structure AND 10 fake-outs (wick-above-high / wick-below-low that a noob would mistake for a BOS) — 20 examples total, done before the next video [29:20-30:29]. He also mentions a separate "how to spot a break of structure in the market" video [29:43].

## Codex interpretation

- **BOS = confirmed trend shift, gated on a candle BODY CLOSE through the most recent opposing swing.** This is the single most testable primitive in the strategy. Formalize as: given a defined trend and its most-recent swing point, a BOS fires only when a candle's CLOSE (not high/low/wick) is strictly beyond that swing extreme — above the most recent swing high for an up-BOS, below the most recent swing low for a down-BOS. Wick penetration alone = NO signal (explicit, repeated, and written-down by TJR — treat as high confidence).
- **Swing-point definition is a 2-candle fractal, NOT a classic 3-bar pivot.** TJR: a high = up-candle then down-candle; low = down-candle then up-candle; the swing price = the extreme wick across those 2 candles. This is looser than the common 3-bar (higher-than-N-neighbors) definition. FLAG: coding a standard fractal detector would diverge from TJR's stated definition. Confirm which he actually trades from (the drawn examples may use eyeballed structural swings, not literal 2-candle patterns) — see ambiguities.
- **Direction-conditional watch is deterministic:** uptrend ⇒ monitor most-recent LOW for a close-below (that would be a down-BOS/CHoCH); downtrend ⇒ monitor most-recent HIGH for a close-above. Only the single most-recent qualifying swing is armed; older swings are dropped. This is a clean state machine: {trend, armed_swing} → close-through → flip trend + re-arm.
- **BOS vs MSS/CHoCH:** In THIS lesson TJR treats "break of structure" and "market structure shift" as SYNONYMS and always describes the *reversal* case (uptrend→downtrend and vice-versa). He does NOT draw the SMC distinction where BOS=continuation (break in trend direction) and CHoCH/MSS=reversal. His BOS-to-downside from an uptrend is functionally what SMC calls a CHoCH. This is a JARGON DIVERGENCE from standard SMC — do not assume his "BOS" means continuation. Flag for the coder (ambiguities + verification-questions).
- **Timeframe is a required parameter, undefined numerically.** BOS is relative to the timeframe watched; a daily BOS against a still-bullish weekly is a retracement, not a bias flip. The detector must be timeframe-scoped and ideally HTF-filtered (weekly/monthly dominate). TJR gives the hierarchy qualitatively but no concrete "trade only if BOS aligns with HTF" rule number.
- **Fake BOS = liquidity sweep.** A wick beyond a swing that does NOT close beyond it is the canonical fake-out and is explicitly the same object as a liquidity sweep (Lesson 08). The body-close gate is precisely what separates a real BOS from a sweep. This links Lesson 06 (BOS/confirmation) ↔ Lesson 08 (liquidity/sweep): sweep = wick-through-no-close; BOS = close-through. In the full playbook (per Lesson 08): sweep of liquidity THEN BOS in the opposite direction = entry trigger.
- **Continuation is real but underspecified.** TJR shows uptrends continuing through repeated up-BOS ("higher high higher low… continues") — so BOS also occurs *in* trend direction as continuation. But he never separates continuation-BOS from reversal-BOS terminologically. The armed-swing model handles both, but labeling (continuation vs reversal) needs a rule the lesson doesn't give.
- **No numeric thresholds** for penetration depth, minimum body size, or which timeframe. All deferred/undefined — see ambiguities.md.
